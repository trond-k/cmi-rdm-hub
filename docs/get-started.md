# Start here

*This hub is a practical reference for managing research data at CMI. It follows your project from the first idea through to long-term preservation and reuse. You don't need to read it cover to cover; find the stage you're at now and start there.*

## What this hub does

Research data management is not a single task you complete and move on from. It is a set of decisions, spread across the life of a project, about what data you collect, how you protect it, where you store it, and what happens to it when the project ends. Getting these decisions right matters for the quality of your research, for the safety of your participants, and for whether anyone (including you) can build on this work later.

This hub gives you guidance tailored to CMI's research context: multi-country projects, sensitive topics, partnerships in the Global South, and the regulatory frameworks that apply to them. It is not a generic data management manual. Where CMI has specific infrastructure, policies, or constraints, the guidance reflects that.

## How the hub is structured

The content is organised around a twelve-stage lifecycle, grouped into three phases:

### Before the project

You are designing, applying for funding, or setting up the project.

- [**Frame**](lifecycle-1-frame.md) – define what data you need, check what already exists, and screen for sensitivity
- [**Fund**](lifecycle-2-fund.md) – align your data plans with funder requirements and budget for data management
- [**Plan**](lifecycle-3-plan.md) – formalise data handling in a Data Management Plan, assign roles, and address legal and ethical requirements

### During the project

You are actively collecting, processing, and analysing data.

- [**Collect**](lifecycle-4-collect.md) – gather data using sound protocols, with proper consent and quality controls
- [**Store**](lifecycle-5-store.md) – keep data secure, backed up, and organised
- [**Process**](lifecycle-6-process.md) – clean, validate, and transform raw data, including pseudonymisation where needed
- [**Analyse**](lifecycle-7-analyse.md) – document your analytical decisions so others can follow your reasoning

### After the project

You are wrapping up, publishing, and ensuring long-term access.

- [**Publish**](lifecycle-8-publish.md) – make your outputs citable and link data, code, and publications together
- [**Preserve**](lifecycle-9-preserve.md) – deposit data for long-term storage in formats that will remain usable
- [**Discover**](lifecycle-10-discover.md) – apply metadata standards so others can find your data
- [**Access**](lifecycle-11-access.md) – define who can use your data and under what conditions
- [**Share and Reuse**](lifecycle-12-share-and-reuse.md) – license your data and document it well enough for responsible reuse

### Cross-cutting guidance

Some topics run across multiple stages. These pages address them in one place rather than repeating the same advice throughout:

- [Reproducibility and transparency](reproducibility-and-transparency.md) – documenting tools, decisions, and AI use; preregistration
- [File and folder naming](file-and-folder-naming.md) – conventions that prevent confusion as files multiply
- [The data inventory](data-inventory.md) – a running register of every dataset in your project

## Where to start

Your entry point depends on where you are right now.

**Starting a new project?** Begin with [Frame](lifecycle-1-frame.md). It will help you think through what data you actually need and flag potential problems before they become expensive. Then move to [Fund](lifecycle-2-fund.md) and [Plan](lifecycle-3-plan.md) as the project takes shape.

**Already collecting data?** Go to [Store](lifecycle-5-store.md) and [Process](lifecycle-6-process.md) to check that your current setup is sound. If you don't yet have a [data inventory](data-inventory.md), now is a good time to build one; it gets harder the longer you wait.

**Approaching the end of a project?** Start with [Publish](lifecycle-8-publish.md) and [Preserve](lifecycle-9-preserve.md). These stages have dependencies on earlier decisions, so you may need to work backwards through [Access](lifecycle-11-access.md) and [Discover](lifecycle-10-discover.md) to fill gaps.

**Writing a funding application?** [Fund](lifecycle-2-fund.md) covers what funders expect and how to budget for data management. You will also want to skim [Frame](lifecycle-1-frame.md) to make sure your data strategy is realistic before you commit to it in a proposal.

**Curious about the bigger picture?** [CMI's institutional context](cmi-institutional-context.md) explains how CMI's research portfolio shapes its approach to data management. [Foundations of data sharing](foundations-of-data-sharing.md) covers the historical and ethical arguments underpinning open science and data governance.

## Using this hub with AI assistants

You can also [use this hub with an AI assistant](using-this-hub-with-ai.md). Give your assistant the hub's content as context and ask questions about data management in your specific project situation. The page includes ready-made prompts for common scenarios.

## Two things worth doing early

Whatever stage you are at, two actions pay for themselves quickly:

1. **Start a [data inventory](data-inventory.md).** Even a simple spreadsheet listing your datasets, their locations, and their sensitivity levels gives you and your team a shared picture of what exists and where the risks are.

2. **Adopt a [file naming convention](file-and-folder-naming.md).** Agree on it with your team before files start multiplying. Renaming hundreds of files later is tedious work that nobody wants to do.
