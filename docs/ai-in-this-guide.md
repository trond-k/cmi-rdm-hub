# How AI was used in this guide

*This hub advises researchers to document their use of AI tools openly and precisely. The same standard applies here. This page describes how AI contributed to producing the RDM Hub, what controls were applied, and what that means for you as a reader.*

## Why we disclose this

The [Reproducibility and transparency](reproducibility-and-transparency.md) page asks you to record every AI tool you use in research: the tool, the version, the task, and how you handled the output. It would be inconsistent to set that standard without meeting it ourselves. This disclosure is not an apology for using AI, nor a promotion of it. It is a factual account so you can assess the content accordingly.

## How AI contributed

AI tools were used at several stages of the hub's production:

- **Content drafting.** AI generated initial drafts of pages based on human-authored outlines, source documents (funder policies, regulatory texts, institutional guidelines), and the hub's style guide.
- **Structural design.** AI helped develop the content architecture, including the lifecycle stage framework, navigation structure, and page templates.
- **Research synthesis.** AI summarised funder requirements, Norwegian data protection guidance, and repository policies from primary sources provided by the editor.
- **Editing and consistency.** AI was used for copyediting, British English enforcement, and checking adherence to the style guide across pages.

The workflow was iterative: a human editor provided outlines, reviewed AI output, corrected errors, and re-prompted where the result fell short. No page was published from a single unreviewed AI pass.

## What AI did not do

- **Editorial judgement.** Decisions about what to include, what to emphasise, and how to frame contested topics (data sovereignty, Global South research contexts, GDPR interpretation) were made by human editors drawing on CMI's institutional knowledge.
- **Source verification.** All citations, URLs, regulatory references, and factual claims were checked by a human editor against primary sources. AI-generated references were treated as unverified drafts until confirmed.
- **Institutional policy.** CMI's positions and recommendations reflect human decisions, not AI output.
- **Final sign-off.** Every page was reviewed and approved by a human editor before publication.

## Limitations you should be aware of

No production process is without risk, and AI-assisted content carries specific ones:

- **Residual errors.** AI models generate plausible but sometimes incorrect information. Despite verification, errors may remain. If you spot one, please let us know.
- **Framing bias.** AI models reflect patterns in their training data. Subtle biases in emphasis or framing may persist despite editorial review.
- **Currency.** AI model knowledge has a training cutoff date, and the fields this hub covers (funder policies, GDPR guidance, repository services, AI governance itself) change frequently. The "Last reviewed" date on each page indicates when content was last verified.

## A model you can adapt

If you are writing up your own research and need to disclose AI use, this page is one example of how to do it. The [documentation checklist](reproducibility-and-transparency.md) on the Reproducibility and transparency page provides a more concise, project-level format. The key principle is the same: say what tool you used, what it did, what you did with the output, and what the reader should know.
