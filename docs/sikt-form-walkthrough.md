# Sikt form walkthrough

*Use this page with the [Sikt form](https://meldeskjema.sikt.no) open. It follows the 13 form sections in order and shows CMI's typical answers. For the short overview, quick defaults table, and common mistakes, see [Prepare a Sikt notification](sikt-notification.md).*

## 1. Personal data types

Check everything that applies. The common mistake is under-reporting, especially for qualitative projects where identifiers and special-category data emerge through the interview even when they are not the explicit topic. If in doubt, check the box. It is better to declare and explain than to omit and have Sikt flag it later.

Most CMI projects need at least `Name`, `Contact information`, `Voice on audio recordings`, and `Background information that, when combined, can be used to identify an individual`. For special categories, `Health data`, `Ethnicity`, and `Political opinions` are the most common in CMI research.

??? note "All form options: general categories (bold = common for CMI)"
    - **Name**
    - 11-digit personal identifier or other national ID number
    - Date of birth
    - **Contact information** (address, email, phone number)
    - Online identifiers (IP address, cookies, device identifiers; relevant if using survey providers)
    - People in images or video recordings
    - **Voice on audio recordings**
    - Electronic location data (GPS, sensors, geographic data from apps)
    - **Background information that, when combined, can be used to identify an individual**
    - Other personal information

??? note "All form options: special categories (bold = common for CMI)"
    - **Health data** (includes mental health, wellbeing, disability, substance use, social security status)
    - **Ethnicity** (affiliation with an ethnic group or national minority)
    - **Political opinions** (political views, party affiliation, political activities)
    - Religious beliefs
    - Philosophical beliefs
    - Sex life
    - Trade union membership
    - Genetic data
    - Biometric data
    - Criminal offences

    Special-category data often arises incidentally in qualitative research. If your topic touches governance, conflict, migration, or health, you will likely need at least one.

## 2. Data controller

For most projects, CMI is the data controller and the named project leader is the PI or another formally affiliated lead.

The point that usually needs actual judgement is whether another institution is a joint controller or only a collaborator. Treat a partner as a joint controller when it genuinely co-determines the purposes and means of processing. Treat a contractor or service provider as a processor when it works on CMI's instructions.

Select 'Yes' for joint data controllers if a partner university independently collects data as part of the project or both institutions co-designed the research and share decision-making over the data. Select 'No' if the partner is a contracted data collection firm, provides existing data without co-determining your research, or supplies individual research assistants who work under your instruction.

## 3. Project information

- Use the real project title and give a short, concrete summary.
- Upload the full project description if the project is complex. For large RCN or EU proposals, uploading the description is recommended.
- For funding, select the source that applies.
- Use 'Research/PhD project' as the academic level for ordinary CMI research projects.

??? note "All form options: funding source"
    - The Research Council of Norway
    - Public authorities (research commissioned by a ministry)
    - Other (Norad, EU, bilateral agreements, private actors, etc.)

??? note "All form options: academic level"
    - **Research/PhD project**
    - Master's
    - Bachelor's
    - One-year programme

## 4. Purpose

For most CMI projects, the primary purpose is 'Research or student paper'.

In the free-text description, explain what the project is studying and why personal data is necessary for that purpose. Keep it specific. Sikt wants to understand why identifiable or linkable data is needed, not just that the project is interesting.

Select the range for total number of data subjects. For most qualitative projects this is '1–99'. For larger survey or mixed-methods projects, estimate the total across all participant groups.

??? note "All form options: primary purpose"
    - **Research or student paper**
    - Health research (only if the project falls under the Health Research Act)
    - Internal quality assurance in healthcare services
    - External quality assurance in healthcare services
    - Quality assurance in education
    - Establishing a clinical quality registry
    - Establishing a population-based health survey
    - Establishing other type of research registry
    - Other

??? note "All form options: total number of data subjects"
    - **1–99** (most qualitative projects)
    - 100–999
    - 1000–4999
    - 5000–9999
    - 10 000–49 999
    - 50 000–100 000
    - 100 000+

## 5. Legal basis

For a typical CMI project:

- Article 6 basis: 'Research in the public interest'
- Article 9 basis for special-category data: 'Research in the public interest'

Sikt's own guidance states that '[in many projects, public interest will be a more appropriate basis for processing](https://sikt.no/en/legal-basis-personal-data-processing-research)' than consent. This applies to most CMI research: long-duration or complex projects, fieldwork with vulnerable groups, power imbalances, and situations where documenting consent is impractical or culturally inappropriate.

!!! warning "Legal basis is not the same as participant agreement"
    Participants still need clear information and must take part voluntarily. But the legal basis for processing their data is normally public interest, not consent. Your choice here affects how you describe information to participants, how you handle data subject rights, and what happens at project end.

??? note "All form options: Article 6 legal basis"
    - Consent (must be voluntary, specific, informed, unambiguous, documented, and as easy to withdraw as to give)
    - **Research in the public interest** (requires safeguards to reduce risk for data subjects)
    - Legal obligation (when special legislation requires the processing)
    - Purposes of legitimate interests (when necessary for the data controller's own interests)

??? note "All form options: Article 9 legal basis (special categories)"
    - Explicit consent (must be exceptionally clear)
    - **Research in the public interest** (requires written assessment that societal benefit outweighs risk)
    - Data which are manifestly made public by the data subject
    - Purposes of preventative or occupational medicine (quality assurance)

## 6. Samples

Create a separate sample for each meaningfully different participant group. Do not collapse very different groups into one entry if they differ in recruitment, vulnerability, or data type.

Typical examples:

- officials and experts
- household survey respondents
- community participants
- frontline workers or partner staff

For each sample, the form asks how data will be collected, which vulnerability categories apply, and how participants will be informed.

Check relevant vulnerability categories honestly, including 'Persons residing in countries outside the EU/EEA' for essentially every CMI fieldwork project. This does not block the project. It tells Sikt that the risk context is real and has been considered.

For information to the sample, select 'Yes' and 'Written (on paper or electronically)' for most projects. Use 'Oral' only where written delivery is genuinely inappropriate. You will be asked to upload the information letter. Sikt provides [templates for information letters](https://sikt.no/en/study-or-research/notification-form-personal-data/information-for-participants), including a version specifically for [research projects using public interest as legal basis](https://cms.sikt.no/sites/default/files/2024-10/Template%20for%20information%20letter%20-%20Research%20project%20(public%20interest%20as%20legal%20basis).docx).

??? note "All form options: data collection method"
    - **Personal interview**
    - **Group interview**
    - **Online survey**
    - Paper-based survey
    - **Participant observation**
    - Non-participant observation
    - Field experiment/field intervention
    - Web-based experiment
    - Tests for pedagogical research/psychological tests
    - Medical examination and/or physical tests

??? note "All form options: data collection method (continued)"
    - Human biological material
    - Social media, open forum
    - Social media, closed forum
    - Discussion board/forum for online newspapers/online debates
    - Register
    - Big data
    - Medical records
    - Biobank
    - **Workshop**
    - Reflection paper, student work, etc.
    - Data from another research project
    - Other

??? note "All form options: vulnerability categories"
    - Patients, disabled people, or sick people
    - **Vulnerable groups** (inmates, benefit recipients, substance abusers, ethnic minorities, homeless individuals, asylum seekers, transgender individuals, people with intellectual disabilities, the elderly, individuals with dementia, or children)
    - **Persons residing in countries outside the EU/EEA**
    - Adults (18 years +) who are not able to give consent

## 7. Third persons

Third persons are people that your participants talk about, not direct participants themselves. This is common in CMI research: an interview about governance may involve the participant describing officials or community members by name or in identifiable terms.

Select 'Yes' if participants will describe identifiable individuals who are not themselves participating. You will then need to describe who the third persons are, which personal data categories apply to them, and whether they will be informed or consent.

In most CMI research, informing third persons is not practicable. Explain this straightforwardly: their identities are not known to the researchers prior to data collection, and references will be minimised or anonymised in transcripts and outputs.

## 8. Documentation

The form asks: 'How can data subjects get access to their personal data or have their personal data corrected or deleted?'

Keep the answer operational. A typical CMI answer: 'Participants can contact the project leader [name, email] or CMI's data protection contact [email] to request access, correction, or deletion. Requests will be handled under the applicable research and data protection rules.'

Under the public interest basis, the right to erasure may be limited where deletion would seriously impair the research objectives, but CMI's default practice is to accommodate requests.

## 9. Security measures

If you use pseudonymisation, select 'Yes' for storing identifiable data separately and say that identifiers or contact details are stored separately from the working research material. If the material is inherently identifiable (such as audio recordings), select 'No' and describe the safeguard strategy instead.

For a typical CMI setup, the technical measures normally include:

- Encrypted storage
- Encrypted transmission
- Restricted access
- Multi-factor authentication

For where data is processed, select 'Hardware' for CMI's M365 environment and 'Mobile devices' if fieldwork involves tablets or phones. Do not select 'Private services'.

!!! tip "The data-flow description is the most important free-text field"
    Describe the path clearly: how data is collected, where it is uploaded or stored, who can access it, how transcription or analysis happens, and what happens to local copies, keys, and recordings at the end. A typical CMI fieldwork example:

    'Data is collected on encrypted tablets/recorders during fieldwork in [country]. Recordings are uploaded to CMI's M365 environment (Teams/SharePoint with restricted access) via encrypted connection. Local copies are deleted after confirmed upload. Transcription is performed by [project staff / named service with DPA]. The anonymisation key is stored in a separate M365 location accessible only to the PI.'

??? note "All form options: technical and practical measures"
    - Continuous anonymisation (deleting, grouping, or rewriting identifying information as you go)
    - **Encrypted transmission**
    - **Encrypted storage**
    - Record of changes (logging who modified data and when)
    - **Multi-factor authentication**
    - **Restricted access**
    - Access log (logging who accessed data and when)
    - Other security measures (locking physical documents, keypad locks on mobile devices, hard drive partitioning, etc.)

??? note "All form options: where data is processed"
    - **Hardware** (the data controller's research server, designated area, etc.)
    - **Mobile devices** (collection, storage, or analysis on devices without network connection)
    - Physically isolated hardware (not connected to any network)
    - Private services (personal OneDrive, Dropbox, iCloud, Google Drive; not permitted under CMI policy)

## 10. Recipients

List the people or organisations that will actually access personal data:

- Project leader
- Internal co-workers (other CMI staff on the project team)
- External co-workers/collaborators inside the EU/EEA (European partner institutions)
- Data processor (survey firms, transcription services, or any external service processing personal data on your behalf; requires a data processing agreement)

For many CMI projects, third-country transfer is unavoidable because partners, processors, or fieldwork operations involve organisations outside the EU/EEA. If so, select 'Yes' and add one entry per organisation with the transfer basis that matches the real arrangement:

- 'Decision on adequate level of protection (Art. 45(3))' applies only if the recipient country has an EU adequacy decision (UK, Japan, South Korea, Canada, etc.)
- 'Covered by necessary safeguards (Art. 46)' applies when covered by a joint controllership agreement, DPA, or standard contractual clauses
- 'Derogations for specific situations (Art. 49)' includes the research derogation for transfers necessary for important reasons of public interest

The important thing is consistency between the transfer basis, the recipient, and the agreement or arrangement you describe. Use the comment field to briefly explain each transfer arrangement.

## 11. End of project

Use the project dates to describe the real data-processing period, not only the formal project period on paper. The project start is when personal data processing actually begins (first contact with participants). The project end is when identifiable data is deleted, anonymised, or otherwise handled as described.

For what happens to data, most CMI projects select 'Personal data will be anonymised'. Check the measures that apply:

- The identification key will be deleted
- Personally identifiable information will be removed, re-written or categorised
- Any sound or video recordings will be deleted

If you plan to retain identifiable data temporarily for documentation or archiving purposes, select 'Personal data will be stored temporarily' and describe what will be retained, for how long, and for what purpose (such as archiving for data sharing and reuse, or documentation purposes).

For publications, select 'No' for identifiable in publications unless participants have specifically agreed to be named.

??? note "All form options: what happens to data at end of project"
    - **Personal data will be anonymised (deleting or rewriting identifiable data)**
    - All data will be deleted (deleting raw data)
    - Personal data will be stored temporarily (you specify when it will be anonymised/deleted and the storage purpose)
    - Personal data will be stored long-term or permanently (for archiving or data sharing with reuse)

## 12. Additional information

Use this field only for context that genuinely helps the assessor, such as unusual field conditions, agreements in preparation, or an atypical data flow. A few sentences is usually enough.

## 13. Privacy risk and DPIA

The form lists risk factors and asks whether a Data Protection Impact Assessment is needed. You can answer 'Yes', 'No', or 'Not sure'. If two or more factors apply, a DPIA is generally expected.

Factors that commonly apply to CMI projects:

- **Highly personal information** (special categories such as health, ethnicity, political opinions)
- **Vulnerable groups** (refugees, people in conflict zones, economically marginalised communities)
- **Persons residing outside the EU/EEA** (most CMI fieldwork)

If a DPIA is required, the form asks detailed follow-up questions. Answer proportionally and concretely. The goal is not to claim that no risk exists. The goal is to show that the project understands and manages the risk. If you cannot answer all the questions, Sikt's own guidance says to leave fields empty and submit; an adviser will contact you.

??? note "All form options: risk factors"
    - **Highly personal information** (family relationships, finances, health, political opinions)
    - A large scale (many participants and/or many variables per individual)
    - Long duration (processing for more than 10 years)
    - Regular processing (repeated data collection, e.g. every three years)
    - Hindering data subjects from exercising their rights
    - Matching or combining datasets beyond reasonable expectation
    - Innovative use or application of new technology (machine learning, welfare technology, etc.)
    - Systematic monitoring (GPS data, observation over time)
    - Evaluation or profiling (assessment of job performance, finances, health, interests, behaviour)
    - Automated decision-making with legal implications

??? note "DPIA follow-up fields"
    - **Consulting data subjects**: describe any co-design, advisory panels, or community consultations. If not feasible, explain why.
    - **Transparent and fair**: describe how participants are informed and any adaptations for vulnerable groups (simplified language, oral delivery, local language translation).
    - **How information is provided**: explain the delivery method. If no individual notification, describe alternatives.
    - **Purpose clearly defined**: note that qualitative research is exploratory, but the overall purpose is defined.
    - **All data necessary**: explain why personal data is needed and why anonymised or aggregate data would not serve.
    - **Data accuracy**: for interview data, accuracy is ensured through the research relationship. For surveys, describe validation measures.
    - **Storage period**: reference the project's planned retention period.
    - **Responsibility and roles**: name joint controllers and data processors and reference agreements.
    - **Access and systems**: list approximately how many people, from which organisations, through which systems.
