# CMI research data in external repositories

*CMI does not operate a centralised institutional data repository. Instead, research data from CMI projects is deposited in discipline-specific and general-purpose repositories, often through partner institutions. This page maps where CMI research data can currently be found, what kinds of data are available, and where to deposit your own data. It is a practical starting point for discovering existing CMI datasets and for choosing where to share yours.*

## The current landscape

CMI's research is predominantly qualitative and policy-oriented: fieldwork interviews, focus groups, policy analysis, and case studies in development, governance, conflict, and migration. Much of this data involves sensitive personal information or politically sensitive contexts, which limits what can be openly deposited. Where CMI researchers do share data openly, it tends to be through domain-specific platforms rather than general-purpose repositories.

This is not unusual for an institute of CMI's size and disciplinary profile. It does, however, mean that finding CMI-produced data requires knowing where to look.

## Where CMI data is available

### ICTD datasets

The most substantial collection of openly available data connected to CMI is hosted by the [International Centre for Tax and Development](https://www.ictd.ac/) (ICTD), which CMI co-founded and co-directs. CMI researcher Odd-Helge Fjeldstad is a Research Director of ICTD. The ICTD hosts eight major open datasets:

| Dataset | Description | Status |
|---|---|---|
| [Government Revenue Dataset](https://www.ictd.ac/dataset/government-revenue-dataset/) | Tax and non-tax revenue data for over 200 countries | Updated November 2025 |
| [Tax Treaties Explorer](https://www.ictd.ac/dataset/tax-treaties-explorer/) | Interactive tool for exploring bilateral tax treaties | Active |
| Afrobarometer Tax Data | Public attitudes to taxation across African countries | Active |
| Ethiopian Taxpayer Compliance Data | Administrative data on taxpayer compliance in Ethiopia | Available |
| Mexican Taxpayer Compliance Data | Administrative data on taxpayer compliance in Mexico | Available |
| NESG Nigeria Tax & Subsidy Perception Dataset | Survey data on tax and subsidy perceptions in Nigeria | Available |

These datasets are openly accessible through the [ICTD dataset page](https://www.ictd.ac/dataset/) and represent the most visible example of CMI-affiliated open data.

### OSF (Open Science Framework)

CMI does not have an institutional presence on [OSF](https://osf.io/). There is no branded CMI portal aggregating researcher projects, and OSF is not widely used across the institute. Individual researchers have, however, deposited data and pre-registrations:

- **Charlotte Ringdal** hosts replication data for 'Who decides in the household when risk preferences conflict?' (with Ben D'Exelle, *Review of Economics of the Household*, 2025) at [osf.io/wqhyb](https://osf.io/wqhyb).
- **Viola Asri** has a pre-registration at [osf.io/wsxd9](https://osf.io/wsxd9) for a study tracking publication outcomes of trials registered on the AEA RCT Registry.

### Harvard Dataverse

- **Charlotte Ringdal** deposited replication data for 'Household bargaining and spending on children' (with Ingrid Hoem Sjursen, *Economica*, 2021) at [Harvard Dataverse](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/) (DOI: 10.7910/DVN/TX2N3C).

### AEA RCT Registry

Several CMI researchers have registered randomised trials with pre-analysis plans:

- **Viola Asri:** Trial AEARCTR-0004510, evaluating a state-capacity-building intervention for targeting extreme poverty in Bangladesh (80 unions, approximately 4,400 observations). A second trial (AEARCTR-0008717) examines preferences in the Indian marriage market.
- **Carlo Koos:** Trial AEARCTR-0005525, a cluster-randomised trial on whether development aid affects local democratisation in Sub-Saharan Africa (with Anselm Hager). A second trial (AEARCTR-0009818) studies the effects of information on development interventions in fragile states.

### RePEc / IDEAS

CMI Working Papers are listed in the [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/) economics research archive. The institutional handle is `RePEc:edi:cmiiino`. This provides bibliographic visibility for CMI's economics-oriented working papers, though it does not host datasets directly.

### DataverseNO

[DataverseNO](https://dataverse.no/) is Norway's national research data repository and accepts deposits from Norwegian institutions. CMI, based in Bergen, is eligible to deposit here. At the time of writing, no dedicated CMI collection exists on DataverseNO, but it remains a viable option for Norwegian-funded projects requiring data deposit.

### CMI's own website

[cmi.no](https://www.cmi.no/) hosts reports, working papers, and policy briefs. These are publications rather than structured research datasets with persistent identifiers, but they represent a significant body of research output.

## What is notably absent

CMI does not currently have a dedicated collection on [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/), [Figshare](https://figshare.com/), [Dryad](https://datadryad.org/), or the [Sikt Research Data Archive](https://sikt.no/en/tjenester/arkivere-data). There is no institutional OSF portal.

This partly reflects the nature of CMI's research. Qualitative fieldwork data involving interviews in conflict-affected or politically sensitive contexts often cannot be openly deposited without risking participant safety. The GDPR's requirements around personal data add further constraints. Where data sharing is possible, it has happened through individual researcher initiative rather than institutional infrastructure.

!!! note "This is a starting point, not a problem"
    The absence of a centralised repository is common among social science research institutes of CMI's size. What matters is that data sharing decisions are made deliberately, that shareable data is deposited in appropriate repositories, and that data which cannot be shared is documented with sufficient metadata to support discoverability. See the [Discover](lifecycle-10-discover.md) and [Access](lifecycle-11-access.md) stages for guidance.

## Choosing where to deposit your data

If you are planning to share data from a CMI project, the choice of repository depends on your discipline, funder requirements, and the nature of the data.

| Consideration | Recommended repository |
|---|---|
| Economics, randomised evaluations | [AEA RCT Registry](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/) for registrations; discipline-specific repository for data |
| Governance, political science experiments | [EGAP Registry](https://egap.org/registry/) for registrations |
| Norwegian-funded projects | [DataverseNO](https://dataverse.no/) or [Sikt Research Data Archive](https://sikt.no/en/tjenester/arkivere-data) |
| Replication data for publications | [OSF](https://osf.io/), [Harvard Dataverse](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/), or [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/) |
| Sensitive social science data | [Sikt Research Data Archive](https://sikt.no/en/tjenester/arkivere-data) (supports restricted access) |
| Tax and development data | [ICTD](https://www.ictd.ac/dataset/) (if connected to ICTD programmes) |
| General-purpose, any discipline | [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/) (free, DOI minting, any file type) |

For guidance on access levels, metadata, and long-term preservation, see the [Preserve](lifecycle-9-preserve.md), [Discover](lifecycle-10-discover.md), and [Access](lifecycle-11-access.md) stages.
