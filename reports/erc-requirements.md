# European Research Council (ERC): Data Management and Open Science Requirements

**Compiled: 2026-03-26 | For: CMI RDM Guidance Hub**

---

## 1. Data Management Plan (DMP) Requirements

### Is a DMP mandatory?

Yes. Since 2021 (the start of Horizon Europe), it is **no longer possible for ERC applicants to opt out** of submitting a data management plan. A DMP is mandatory for all ERC frontier research grants (Starting, Consolidator, Advanced, Synergy). The only exception is **ERC Proof of Concept grants**, which are excluded from the Open Research Data requirements.

### When is it due?

| Milestone | What is required |
|---|---|
| **Proposal stage** | A short (1-page) statement on research data management, included in the methodology section under the Excellence criterion. |
| **Within 6 months of grant signature** | A full, detailed DMP submitted as a mandatory project deliverable. |
| **Mid-project** (for projects >12 months) | An updated DMP deliverable. |
| **End of project** | A final updated DMP (where relevant). |

The DMP should be updated whenever significant changes occur -- new data types, changes in consortium composition, or revised sharing plans.

### What template?

The **Horizon Europe DMP template** is the recommended template. It can be downloaded from the EC Funding & Tenders portal (Reference Documents > Templates & forms > Project reporting templates > "Data management plan (HE)"). However, the **ERC does not prescribe a specific format** since disciplinary practices vary widely. Researchers may adapt the template or use a different one, provided they cover the required topics.

**For CMI researchers:** The Sikt DMP tool (plan.research-data.no) includes a Horizon Europe questionnaire that maps to the official template sections. This is the recommended starting point at CMI.

### What must the DMP cover?

The Horizon Europe DMP template has six main sections:

1. **Data summary** -- Types and formats of data generated or reused, purpose, expected size, origin/provenance, and potential value beyond the project.

2. **FAIR data** (four subsections):
   - *Findability* -- Persistent identifiers, naming conventions, keywords, metadata standards, search optimisation.
   - *Accessibility* -- Repository selection, access protocols, any restrictions (justified), metadata availability even when data is restricted.
   - *Interoperability* -- Vocabularies, standards, formats, and methodologies enabling data exchange.
   - *Reusability* -- Documentation (codebooks, methodology descriptions), licensing (CC BY or CC0), quality assurance measures.

3. **Other research outputs** -- Management of software, workflows, protocols, models, or physical outputs (materials, samples, etc.).

4. **Allocation of resources** -- Costs for making data FAIR, how these will be covered, and who is responsible for data management.

5. **Data security** -- Secure storage, backup, recovery, transfer of sensitive data, and long-term preservation provisions.

6. **Ethics** -- Legal and ethical issues affecting data sharing, personal data handling, informed consent, and references to ethics deliverables.

### How does the ERC DMP relate to the general Horizon Europe DMP?

The ERC DMP **is** the Horizon Europe DMP. There is no separate ERC-specific template or set of requirements. The ERC follows the Horizon Europe MGA (specifically Article 17 and Annex 5) for all open science obligations. The ERC's supplementary guidance documents provide interpretation and practical tips but do not add requirements beyond the MGA.

## 2. Open Access to Research Data

### What is the ERC's policy?

The ERC requires that digital research data generated in funded projects be managed according to the principle **"as open as possible, as closed as necessary."** This is the standard Horizon Europe rule under Article 17 of the MGA.

Key obligations:

- **Deposit research data** in a trusted repository by the end of the project (earlier if data underlies a publication).
- **Provide open access** to the deposited data by default.
- **License data** under **CC BY (latest version)** or **CC0**, or equivalent rights-waiving instruments.
- **Provide metadata** under **CC0**, following FAIR principles, including machine-actionable information.
- **Include funding metadata**: Horizon Europe programme name, ERC grant number, and grant acronym.

### Legitimate grounds for restricting access

Data may be kept closed if open access would conflict with:

- Legitimate commercial exploitation interests
- Data protection, privacy, or confidentiality requirements
- Security rules
- Intellectual property rights
- Other obligations in the Grant Agreement

All restrictions must be **documented and justified in the DMP**.

### How does this differ from general Horizon Europe rules?

It does not differ. ERC grants follow the same Horizon Europe data obligations. The ERC does not impose stricter open data requirements than the MGA baseline.

### ERC Proof of Concept exception

ERC Proof of Concept grants are **excluded** from open research data requirements. This is because PoC grants are focused on commercial exploitation potential, where open data could conflict with IP protection.

## 3. Open Access to Publications

### ERC/Horizon Europe publication requirements

The ERC has historically been a strong advocate for open access to publications, and under Horizon Europe the requirements are more demanding than under H2020:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Immediate open access** | All peer-reviewed publications must be deposited in a trusted repository **at or before the time of publication**. |
| **Licensing** | Publications must carry a **CC BY licence** (or equivalent). For monographs and books, **CC BY-NC** or **CC BY-ND** are also acceptable. |
| **No embargo period** | Under Horizon Europe, there is no permissible embargo. Immediate open access is required. (This is stricter than H2020, which allowed 6- or 12-month embargoes.) |
| **Metadata** | Must be open (CC0), machine-actionable, and include persistent identifiers. Must include the funding programme, ERC grant number, and acronym. |
| **Rights retention** | Beneficiaries must retain sufficient IP rights to allow immediate deposit under CC BY. |

### What version must be deposited?

- If publishing in a **fully open access journal**: deposit the Version of Record (VoR) immediately.
- If publishing in a **subscription or hybrid journal**: self-archive the Author Accepted Manuscript (AAM) immediately under CC BY.

### Publishing fee eligibility

| Venue type | APCs/BPCs eligible? |
|---|---|
| Fully open access journals | **Yes** |
| Hybrid/subscription journals | **No** |
| Transformative journals | **No** (no exception) |
| Open access books (first digital edition) | **Yes** |
| Book printing costs | **No** |

Transformative Agreements (e.g., institutional read-and-publish agreements) may cover costs in hybrid journals without charging the grant.

### Recommended repositories for publications

The ERC Scientific Council recommends:

- **Europe PubMed Central** (life sciences) -- free PI accounts available for ERC grantees
- **arXiv** (physical sciences and engineering)
- **OAPEN Library** (books and chapters, humanities/social sciences)

General-purpose repositories such as **Zenodo** are also acceptable.

**Not acceptable**: Academia.edu, ResearchGate, personal websites, cloud storage, or publisher-only websites.

**For CMI researchers:** NVA (Nasjonalt vitenarkiv) is CMI's default publication archive and meets trusted repository criteria.

### Open Research Europe (ORE) platform

The European Commission funds [Open Research Europe](https://open-research-europe.ec.europa.eu/), an open access publishing platform available to all EU-funded researchers including ERC grantees:

- **No publishing fees** for authors (funded by the EC).
- All articles published under **CC BY**.
- Open, transparent peer review.
- Rapid publication (public within days of submission; indexed after peer review approval).
- Authors asked to provide full FAIR access to underlying source data.

This is a viable option for CMI researchers who want to avoid APC costs entirely.

### Funding acknowledgement

All publications must include the standard acknowledgement text:

> "Funded by the European Union (ERC, [Project Acronym], [Grant Number]). Views and opinions expressed are however those of the author(s) only and do not necessarily reflect those of the European Union or the European Research Council Executive Agency. Neither the European Union nor the granting authority can be held responsible for them."

## 4. FAIR Data Principles

### Does the ERC have specific FAIR expectations?

The ERC embraces FAIR as a core principle. The Horizon Europe MGA (Article 17) requires that research data be managed "in line with the FAIR principles" -- Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable.

In practice, this means:

- **Findable**: Assign persistent identifiers (DOIs), use rich metadata, register data in searchable resources.
- **Accessible**: Deposit in trusted repositories with clear access protocols. Metadata must remain accessible even if data is restricted.
- **Interoperable**: Use community-recognised standards, vocabularies, and formats. Enable machine-actionable metadata.
- **Reusable**: Provide clear licensing (CC BY / CC0), thorough documentation, and provenance information.

The ERC does not define a separate FAIR compliance checklist beyond what the Horizon Europe framework provides. The DMP is the primary instrument for demonstrating FAIR compliance.

## 5. Data Sharing and Archiving

### Repository requirements

Repositories must qualify as "trusted" under one of three criteria:

1. **Certification**: CoreTrustSeal, nestor Seal (DIN 31644), or ISO 16363.
2. **Community recognition**: Internationally recognised discipline- or domain-specific repositories.
3. **Alternative criteria**: Meeting standards specified in the Horizon Europe Annotated Grant Agreement.

**Examples of acceptable repositories:**

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Certified/domain-specific | Sikt Research Data Archive (CoreTrustSeal), CESSDA archives, UK Data Archive, ICPSR |
| General-purpose | Zenodo, Figshare, OSF, Dryad |
| Discipline-specific | Europe PMC (life sciences), GenBank, Pangaea, DataverseNO |

**For CMI researchers:** Sikt Research Data Archive is CMI's default and holds CoreTrustSeal certification. It is the recommended first choice. Zenodo and OSF are listed alternatives.

### Timing of data deposit

- Data underlying publications: deposit **by the time of publication**.
- Other project data: deposit **as soon as possible**, no later than **project end**.
- In public emergencies: immediate access to relevant entities is required.
- Exceptional cases may allow post-project deposition.

### Metadata requirements

Metadata must:

- Be open under **CC0** or equivalent.
- Be machine-actionable and structured for automated processing.
- Include: funding programme (Horizon Europe), ERC grant number, grant acronym.
- Use persistent identifiers: DOI for datasets, ORCID for authors, ROR for organisations.
- Follow FAIR principles and discipline-specific metadata standards where applicable.
- Remain accessible **even if the data itself is restricted**.

## 6. History: From Open Research Data Pilot to Mandatory Open Access

| Period | Policy |
|---|---|
| **H2020 2014--2016** | Open Research Data Pilot launched on a voluntary, opt-in basis for selected thematic areas. |
| **H2020 2017--2020** | ORD Pilot extended to **all thematic areas**. Participation by default, but projects could **opt out** at any stage. |
| **Horizon Europe 2021--present** | Open access to research data becomes **mandatory**. **No opt-out** available. The "as open as possible, as closed as necessary" principle replaces the opt-out mechanism. Researchers can restrict specific datasets with documented justification, but cannot opt out of data management obligations entirely. |

### Key changes from H2020 to Horizon Europe

| Aspect | H2020 | Horizon Europe |
|---|---|---|
| DMP | Required if in ORD Pilot; opt-out available | **Mandatory for all projects** |
| Open access to data | Default but with full opt-out | **Mandatory**; restrictions case-by-case with justification |
| Open access to publications | Embargo allowed (6 or 12 months) | **Immediate**, no embargo |
| Publication licensing | No specific licence mandated | **CC BY** mandatory (CC BY-NC/ND for books) |
| Data licensing | Flexible | **CC BY or CC0** mandatory |
| Metadata | Not specified | **CC0**, machine-actionable, FAIR |
| Hybrid journal APCs | Eligible | **Not eligible** |

## 7. Recent Policy Developments (2024--2026)

### ERC Working Group on Open Science -- dissolved April 2025

The ERC Scientific Council's Working Group on Open Science was formally dissolved on 30 April 2025. Its responsibilities transferred to two individual Scientific Council members (Nicola Spaldin and Giovanni Sartor). The Working Group's final output was a 2024 updated study assessing research data and publication repositories against Horizon Europe MGA requirements (published on Zenodo).

### DORA signing

The ERC has signed **DORA** (San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment), reinforcing its position that the scientific quality of research matters rather than the venue of publication. This aligns with open science principles.

### ERC Work Programme 2025

Adopted in 2024. Continues all existing Horizon Europe open science requirements without change. Mentions promoting open science as an ongoing ERC activity.

### ERC Work Programme 2026

Adopted in late 2025. Key structural change: proposals restructured into a concise 5-page Part I (scientific idea and objectives) and a detailed Part II (implementation and methodology). **No substantive changes to open science or data management requirements.** The open science obligations continue to follow the Horizon Europe MGA baseline.

### No ERC-specific tightening beyond Horizon Europe

As of March 2026, the ERC has not introduced open science requirements beyond the Horizon Europe MGA baseline. The ERC's approach is to follow the framework programme rules while advocating for open science through Scientific Council communications and infrastructure such as Open Research Europe.

## Summary: What CMI Researchers with ERC Grants Must Do

### Before the project starts
- Include a 1-page data management statement in the proposal (Excellence section).

### Within 6 months of grant signature
- Submit a detailed DMP as a mandatory deliverable. Use Sikt DMP tool (plan.research-data.no) to draft it using the Horizon Europe questionnaire.
- Set up the Research Data Management work package.

### During the project
- Update the DMP at mid-project and end-of-project (and whenever significant changes occur).
- Deposit peer-reviewed publications in a trusted repository (NVA for CMI) **immediately at publication** under CC BY.
- Do not pay APCs for hybrid journals from the grant.
- Deposit research data underlying publications in a trusted repository (Sikt Research Data Archive) at the time of publication.
- License data under CC BY or CC0.
- Ensure metadata is open (CC0), machine-actionable, and includes PIDs and grant information.

### At project end
- Ensure all research data is deposited in a trusted repository with open access (or documented restrictions).
- Submit the final DMP deliverable.
- Verify all publications and datasets are properly linked, with correct funding acknowledgements and persistent identifiers.
