**Compiled: 2026-03-26 | For: CMI RDM Guidance Hub**

## 1. Data Ownership and Intellectual Property

### Standard Grant Agreement Provisions (Part II General Conditions, Article 9)

The definitive source on IP in Norad-funded projects is **Article 9 of the Part II General Conditions** (Revision 5, October 2024), which applies to all NGO grant agreements under Regime I and II. The key provisions:

**Ownership vests in the Grant Recipient** (Article 9.1):
> "The right of ownership to equipment, consumables and intellectual property rights procured or developed by use of the Grant shall vest in the Grant Recipient or its cooperating partner, unless otherwise stated in the Application."

However, use outside the project requires Donor approval: "significant use of such equipment, consumables and intellectual property rights for purposes outside the Project shall be subject to the Donor's prior approval."

**Public good mandate** (Article 9.2):
> "Intellectual property rights financed in whole or in part from the Grant shall, in the spirit of securing such rights as a common global good, be managed in a way that maximizes their public accessibility and allows the broadest possible use. Material produced as a result of this Grant shall, as far as possible and appropriate, be placed in the public domain for non-commercial use."

**Norad retains a license** (Article 9.3):
> "The Donor shall have a non-exclusive and royalty-free license to use all intellectual property rights procured or developed by the use of the Grant. The Donor may assign this right to any individual or organisation at its own discretion."

### Implications for CMI

- CMI **retains IP ownership** of research data and outputs from Norad-funded projects by default.
- But the agreement creates a strong **normative expectation of public accessibility** -- not just permission but an obligation to maximize openness.
- Norad has a **perpetual, royalty-free, assignable license** to use all IP, meaning they can share or redistribute outputs freely.
- Transfer of IP during the support period must be at market terms and documented (Articles 9.4-9.6).

### Norwegian legal context

Under Norwegian copyright law, the creator(s) are the first owner(s) of copyright in all types of works, including commissioned works. There is no automatic transfer of copyright to the commissioner -- transfer depends on the contractual agreement. This makes the specific grant agreement terms paramount.

## 2. Open Access Requirements

### The Open Policy (Department for Climate, Nature, and the Private Sector)

Norad's most significant open access development is the **Open Policy**, fully effective for grants awarded from **1 January 2025**. It was piloted from 1 October 2024 with selected projects.

The Open Policy mandates unrestricted access and reuse of **all** digital outputs funded wholly or partly by the Department for Climate, Nature, and the Private Sector. It covers four categories:

1. **Datasets**: All collected or produced data, including underlying datasets and metadata, must be openly accessible for reuse.
2. **Technologies**: Source code, algorithms, and models must be published openly for public use.
3. **Research**: Peer-reviewed publications and their underlying data must be made available **without embargo**, ensuring **immediate open access**.
4. **Educational Materials**: Learning resources must be published openly in public repositories.

**Key qualifications:**
- The Open Policy is **not obligatory for all Norad projects** -- it applies only when explicitly stipulated in the grant agreement. It currently covers only the Department for Climate, Nature, and the Private Sector.
- Organizations may only state compliance following **formal confirmation by Norad**.
- Supporting documents include a **Metadata Specification** (v1.0, September 2024; v2.0 referenced as January 2026) and a **Policy Accepted Licenses** document.

### General open access expectations (all Norad grants)

Even outside the formal Open Policy, Norad's Grants Handbook (Section 4 - Application) states:

> "Data collections, research and evaluation reports funded by Norad should be accessible to the public and published in a timely manner."

Partners are also "encouraged to make knowledge and data available to other aid organisations."

### Research strategy (MFA/Norad 2017-2024)

The Research Strategy for the Foreign Service and Norad states: "Research results should be published internationally, preferably on an 'open access' basis." However, this strategy lacks specific data management requirements and is due for renewal.

## 3. Data Management Expectations

### Data Management Plans

**Norad does not currently require formal Data Management Plans (DMPs)** in the way that the Research Council of Norway or Horizon Europe does. There is no standard DMP template or submission requirement in Norad's grant application or reporting framework.

However, Norad requires something functionally adjacent: a **Knowledge Management Plan**.

### Knowledge Management Plans

Applicants must submit a knowledge management plan covering the entire project period. This plan should outline:
- How learning will be used to refine programming and address evidence gaps
- Knowledge products (monitoring activities, evaluations, reviews, or research) clearly connected to the theory of change
- How knowledge gaps will be identified and addressed
- How the organisation will manage the information it generates

Norad's guidance states: "The scope and ambitions of the applicant's knowledge management efforts should be proportional to the size of the project/programme and to the level of certainty that exists surrounding the effectiveness of the intervention."

Applicants are directed to the **Development Learning Lab (DLL)** and **MERLiN network** guide "Making a Knowledge Plan."

### Open Policy Metadata Specification

For projects under the Open Policy, Norad has developed a **Metadata Specification** (v1.0, September 2024) that provides a structured framework for documenting datasets. The specification:
- Requires JSON or YAML format, encoded in UTF-8
- Aims to make data easy to find, ensure consistent quality, and enable combined datasets across Norad-funded projects
- Is designed to improve discoverability across the portfolio

### Data handling during projects

The Part II General Conditions require:
- Accurate accounts and double-entry bookkeeping (Article 7.1)
- Monthly reconciliation of financial data (Article 7.2)
- All project data easily identifiable and traceable (Article 7.3)
- Retention of accounting documentation for at least 5 years after final report approval (Article 7.5)
- Control access: Norad and the Norwegian Auditor General may carry out reviews, audits, or evaluations at any time, and the grant recipient must provide "all information and documentation necessary" with "unrestricted access to any premises, records, goods and documents requested" (Article 6.1-6.2)

## 4. Confidentiality and Publication Restrictions

### Transparency as default (Article 14)

The Part II General Conditions establish a **strong presumption of transparency**:

- **Article 14.1**: The Donor "may make this Agreement and other Project documentation, such as but not limited to, the Application and agreed reports available to the public to promote transparency of the use of public funds."
- **Article 14.2**: "The Grant Recipient shall make the Project documentation, including the Application and all agreed reports, available to anyone upon request."

### Narrow grounds for restricting disclosure

Requests for disclosure may only be denied if:
> "such disclosure is prohibited by confidentiality obligations and/or if it may be detrimental to the Grant Recipient's legitimate interests." (Article 14.2)

This is a deliberately narrow exception. The Grants Handbook implementation guidance further states: "Information remains confidential only where Norwegian law mandates it."

### Recognition requirements (Article 23)

All publications must acknowledge Norwegian government support with the Norway logo and standard disclaimer:
> "This publication has been produced with financial support from Norway. The contents of this publication are the sole responsibility of [grant recipient's name] and can in no way be taken to reflect the views of the Government of Norway."

### What this means for researchers

- Norad's standard framework does **not include publication veto rights**. There is no clause allowing Norad to block or delay publication of research findings.
- The transparency default means Norad can itself make project documentation public.
- Confidentiality restrictions are limited to what Norwegian law requires (e.g., personal data protection, national security) or what would be "detrimental to the Grant Recipient's legitimate interests."
- The IP clauses (Article 9.2) actively push material toward the public domain.

**Note:** Commissioned evaluations and studies procured under public procurement law (anskaffelsesloven) may have different terms than grant agreements. The standard grant conditions analyzed here apply to NGO/research institute grants.

## 5. Evaluation and Reporting Data

### Organizational change: Evaluation moves to Norec (January 2025)

As of **1 January 2025**, independent evaluation of Norwegian development assistance transferred from Norad's Department for Evaluation to **Norec** (Norwegian Agency for Exchange Cooperation), now housing the **Independent Evaluation Department of Norwegian Development Cooperation**. The department relocated to Forde and reports directly to the Secretary Generals of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Ministry of Climate and Environment.

### Evaluation data standards

- Evaluation reports must "clearly document the evidence base and demonstrate a logical, verifiable connection between data, methods, findings, conclusions, and recommendations."
- Evaluation topics are selected based on risk, specificity, and materiality criteria from the Ministry of Finance's Regulations for Financial Management.
- All evaluation reports are **published publicly** at norad.no (historical) and norec.no, together with follow-up letters and ministry response plans.

### Reporting requirements for grant recipients

Annual progress reports must include (Article 2.2):
- Results achieved with comparison to the results framework
- Account of deviations from plans
- Risk reporting, including materialised risks and emerging risks
- Account of cross-cutting issues (anti-corruption, climate/environment, gender equality, human rights)
- Completed research, evaluation, and review reports listed in knowledge management plans, together with the partner's management response

Final reports must additionally include (Article 4.2):
- Assessment of impact on society
- Main lessons learned
- Cost-effectiveness assessment
- Sustainability assessment

### Baseline data and monitoring

Norad expects each indicator to have a baseline value, target, and means of verification. Knowledge products should meet "high quality standards, grounded in sound social science and evaluation methods." Applicants are encouraged to use experimental and quasi-experimental designs, including RCTs where relevant, and to include cost-effectiveness analysis in impact evaluations.

## 6. Aid Transparency and IATI

### Norway's IATI commitment

Norway started publishing to the **IATI Registry** in December 2015. Norad publishes as an IATI publisher (identifier: NO-BRC-971277882). However, publication has been inconsistent:

- Publication frequency is **less than quarterly** (well below the recommended monthly)
- The Publish What You Fund **Aid Transparency Index** has identified gaps in: sub-national location data, contact details, planned/actual dates, and organisational policy documentation
- Norway publishes aid flows to OECD/DAC and publishes all disbursements at resultater.norad.no

### Aid statistics microdata portal

Norad maintains a comprehensive **microdata portal** (resultater.norad.no/microdata) with:
- All official Norwegian ODA statistics from **1960 onwards**
- Downloadable in CSV/Excel format
- Each line corresponds to a payment over a budget post to an aid agreement during a given year
- Detailed information on partner, sector, and amounts (most detailed for projects after 1998)
- Accompanying R package (`noradstats`) for programmatic access

### Relevance to research data

IATI and aid transparency requirements primarily affect **administrative and financial data** about development cooperation activities. They do not directly mandate research data sharing. However, they create an ecosystem expectation of openness:
- Partners are "encouraged to report through IATI"
- Norad practices "enhanced access," publishing grant information and project reports on its website

## 7. Norad's Knowledge Bank (Kunnskapsbanken)

The Knowledge Bank is **not a data repository** -- it is an institutional cooperation framework established in 2018 to coordinate technical cooperation between Norwegian state institutions and partner country institutions.

### Structure and programs

Programs include: Oil for Development (OfD), Fish for Development (FfD), Tax for Development (TfD), Cooperation on Statistics, NORHED (higher education/research), Digitalisation for Development, Anti-corruption and Good Governance, Public Financial Management, Gender Equality for Development (LIKE), Agriculture for Development, and Clean Energy cooperation.

### Knowledge Bank Strategy 2020-2025

The Knowledge Bank operates on the principle that "many partner countries need knowledge rather than funding." It facilitates long-term institutional partnerships between Norwegian agencies and corresponding organizations in partner countries.

### Relevance to research data

The Knowledge Bank is primarily about **institutional capacity building and knowledge transfer**, not research data archiving. However, the Statistics cooperation program involves Norwegian statistical agencies assisting partner countries with "knowledge, data and advice on measures to achieve policy goals." This creates potential data management considerations for CMI projects that intersect with Knowledge Bank programmes.

## 8. Grant vs. Commission Differences

This is a critical distinction for CMI, which receives both grant funding and commissions from Norad.

### Grants (Tilskudd)

Governed by:
- The Grants Handbook for Civil Society and Other Private Actors
- Standard grant agreement (Part I Specific Conditions, Part II General Conditions, Part III Procurement Provisions)
- Norwegian law on state financial management (Reglement for okonomistyring i staten)

Key features:
- IP ownership vests in the grant recipient (Article 9.1)
- Strong public accessibility mandate (Article 9.2)
- Norad retains royalty-free license (Article 9.3)
- Knowledge management plan required
- Transparency default with narrow exceptions
- Partner has relative autonomy in project design and execution

### Commissions/Procurement (Anskaffelser)

Governed by:
- Norwegian Public Procurement Act (Anskaffelsesloven) and associated regulations
- Specific terms of reference and contract
- Norwegian contract law

Key differences likely include:
- **IP ownership may transfer to the commissioner** (Norad/MFA) by default or by contract, since Norwegian law allows this for commissioned works when agreed
- More prescriptive deliverables and timelines
- Norad may have **stronger control over publication timing and content**
- The "public good" IP framing from grant agreements may not apply
- NTNU's general rule provides context: "Unless otherwise agreed for the individual project, the right of ownership of results that are created as commissions passes to the commissioning party"

### Practical implication for CMI

**For commissions**, CMI should carefully negotiate:
- Right to publish independently (academic freedom clause)
- Timing of publication relative to Norad's own use
- Ownership and reuse rights for underlying research data
- Right to deposit data in research data archives

**For grants**, the standard terms are generally favorable for researchers, with IP staying with CMI and a strong push toward open access. But the knowledge management plan and reporting obligations still apply.

## 9. Recent Policy Developments (2024-2026)

### Open Policy launch (2024-2025)

The most significant development. Piloted October 2024, fully effective January 2025 for the Department for Climate, Nature, and the Private Sector. Requires immediate open access to all research publications, datasets, source code, and educational materials. Supported by a metadata specification and accepted licenses framework.

### Evaluation function transfer to Norec (January 2025)

Independent evaluation moved from Norad to Norec, with the department relocating to Forde. This does not directly change data requirements but represents a governance restructuring.

### Knowledge Centre for Development Cooperation (2025-2026)

Norad launched a call for proposals (deadline February 2026) to establish a Knowledge Centre that will "strengthen effective, research-informed development cooperation by supporting civil society organisations with high-quality knowledge, learning systems, and evidence-based approaches." This may create new infrastructure for research data sharing in Norwegian development cooperation.

### Norad's new mandate (August 2024)

Norad received a new mandate from MFA and the Ministry of Climate and Environment requiring that "development cooperation is knowledge-based and cost-effective." This reinforces the emphasis on evidence and data quality.

### Digital Public Goods

Norway co-founded the **Digital Public Goods Alliance (DPGA)** in 2019, co-hosted by UNICEF and Norad. Norway champions open-source software, open data, and open AI systems as tools for development. This creates a broader policy environment favoring open data approaches.

### Norad GitHub expansion

Norad maintains 22 public repositories on GitHub, including:
- `noradstats` -- R package for Norwegian/international development finance data
- `oda-metadata` -- Variable descriptions for aid statistics
- `tst-report-2025-public-data` -- Public datasets for the 2024 statistical report

This signals increasing commitment to programmatic data access and open infrastructure.

## Key Gaps and Uncertainties

1. **No Norad-wide research data policy exists.** The Open Policy currently applies only to the Department for Climate, Nature, and the Private Sector. Other departments (including those commissioning development research) have softer expectations.

2. **Commission/procurement contracts are not publicly standardized** the way grant agreements are. The terms for commissioned evaluations and studies vary by contract. CMI should maintain a library of relevant contract clauses for reference.

3. **No formal DMP requirement.** Unlike the Research Council of Norway, Norad does not require or provide templates for data management plans. The Knowledge Management Plan serves a related but distinct function.

4. **Evaluation data archiving is undefined.** There is no clear requirement or mechanism for archiving underlying evaluation datasets (survey data, qualitative data, monitoring data) in research data repositories. All evaluation reports are published, but underlying data is not systematically preserved.

5. **The Open Policy Metadata Specification** (v2.0, January 2026) was referenced on the Open Policy page but its detailed contents could not be fully extracted. The contact for policy inquiries is Christer Solheim Gundersen (christer.solheim.gundersen@norad.no).
