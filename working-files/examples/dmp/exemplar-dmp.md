# Data management plan: Delivering the Local State (DELGO)

*A fictional exemplar DMP written to the structure of the [CMI DMP template](../../../docs/dmp-cmi.md). Every name, place, and institution is invented. Use it to see what a complete, internally consistent DMP looks like, including the reasoning behind the judgement calls.*

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Purpose and scope

This DMP describes how the DELGO project manages its research data responsibly, ethically, and transparently. The project is committed to the FAIR principles, the GDPR, CMI's institutional policies, and the requirements of the Research Council of Norway. This DMP is a living document and will be revised as the project evolves.

### 1.2 Guiding principles

- **FAIR, transparency, and security:** as open as possible, as closed as necessary.
- **Do-no-harm:** no action, output, or outcome shall adversely affect participants, stakeholders, or third parties.
- **Sensitivity minimisation:** only data essential to the research objectives is collected and retained.
- **Proportionality:** security measures matched to the sensitivity of each dataset, not to the most sensitive item in the project.
- **Access control (least privilege):** role-based access limited to individuals with a justified need.
- **Shared responsibility:** all project members, including partner staff and research assistants, share accountability for lawful and ethical data management.

## 2. Project data summary

### 2.1 About the project

**Full title:** Delivering the Local State: Governance and Public Service Delivery in Decentralised Kanewa

**Acronym:** DELGO

**Funding source:** Research Council of Norway, NORGLOBAL programme

**Grant ID:** 362481

**Project period:** 1 August 2026 – 31 July 2029 (36 months)

**Geographical scope:** Republic of Kanewa (East Africa), fieldwork in Musenda District (peri-urban) and Tarowe District (rural); analysis and coordination in Norway.

DELGO examines how district-level political dynamics shape the delivery of water, health, and primary education services in decentralised Kanewa, comparing two districts with contrasting political settlements to explain why formally identical local government structures produce different service outcomes.

### 2.2 Purpose of data collection

Work package 1 maps governance arrangements through interviews with district officials, councillors, and service managers. Work package 2 examines citizen experience through interviews and focus groups with residents and frontline providers (nurses, teachers, water technicians). Work package 3 traces resource flows through a corpus of public council minutes, district budgets, and service charters.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities

### 3.1 Institutions

**Chr. Michelsen Institute (CMI), Norway,** is the host institution and project coordinator. CMI is responsible for overall project management, data storage and security, GDPR compliance, and repository deposit of open-access publications. CMI's Research Data Management Adviser supports development of this DMP.

**The University of Nyaruma, Kanewa** (Department of Political Science) co-designs instruments, employs two research assistants who conduct and transcribe interviews under project protocols, and co-analyses pseudonymised transcripts. Because it co-determines the purposes and means of processing, it is a joint controller with CMI under a joint controllership agreement (GDPR Art. 26), not a processor.

**Sikt Data Protection Services for Research** assesses the project's processing of personal data through the Sikt notification.

### 3.2 Governance and project groups

A project management team chaired by the PI, Dr Marte Ellingsen (CMI), meets monthly and includes the co-investigator, Dr Joseph Wanjala (University of Nyaruma). Data protection questions are coordinated by CMI's Research Data Management Adviser, escalating to the DPO where needed. Ethics oversight is described in section 4.3.

## 4. Legal and ethical considerations

### 4.1 GDPR compliance

The project processes personal data from interview and focus group participants in Kanewa. The GDPR applies because CMI, a Norwegian controller, determines the purposes of processing; Kanewa's Data Protection Act 2021 also applies to the partner's activities.

**Legal basis for processing.** The lawful basis is public interest (GDPR Art. 6(1)(e)), supported by the Norwegian Personal Data Act §8 and GDPR Art. 89. Interviews will touch on participants' political opinions, so the project also relies on Art. 9(2)(j) for special category data. GDPR consent is not the lawful basis: this is exploratory qualitative research where the scope of relevant data cannot be fully specified in advance, and consent-based withdrawal mid-analysis could seriously impair a three-year comparative design. Recording, naming, and archiving are matters to inform participants about, not consent elements layered on top of public interest.

**Types of personal data.** Direct identifiers (names, contact details, voice recordings), indirect identifiers (job titles, ward names, service roles), and special category data (political opinions, Art. 9). Interviews will incidentally capture third-person data about colleagues; this is minimised at transcription.

**Information and consent.** The information letter is the primary instrument: it seeks ethical consent (voluntary, informed, revocable) and informs participants about recording, whether they may be identified in publications (the default is no; senior officials speaking on the record may choose attribution), archiving of anonymised transcripts for reuse, storage and retention, and their rights. Choices about recording and archiving are presented as project decisions the participant makes in the letter, not as separate GDPR consents. In Tarowe, where literacy varies, the letter is delivered orally in Kanewan with a witnessed record of agreement. Because officials may feel pressure to participate when approached through the district administration, recruitment is direct and the letter states that participation has no bearing on the participant's position.

**Data minimisation and anonymisation.** Only data serving the research questions is collected; demographic details are limited to role, sector, and district. Direct identifiers are pseudonymised at transcription, with the linkage key stored separately and accessible only to the PI and the data steward.

**Data security.** All project data is stored in CMI's M365 environment with encryption, MFA, and role-based access, in a restricted SharePoint site limited to named project members. The subset of interviews with district officials receives additional M365 controls (restricted sharing settings and audit logging) because an official identifiable while criticising their own administration faces real professional risk. The project judges Red with elevated controls sufficient, rather than Black-tier external infrastructure, because Kanewa is politically competitive but not conflict-affected, and the realistic harm is professional rather than physical. In the field, recordings are made on encrypted devices, uploaded to CMI storage within 24 hours, and deleted from devices after verified upload.

**Data subject rights.** Under the public interest basis, rights are modified by Art. 89(2) and the Norwegian Personal Data Act §17: there is no GDPR consent to withdraw (participants may withdraw ethically or object), erasure is limited by Art. 17(3)(d), and portability does not apply. The information letter states that deletion requests can be honoured until anonymisation is complete, expected by 31 December 2028; after that, individual contributions can no longer be isolated.

**International data transfers.** Fieldwork data collected on encrypted devices in Kanewa and uploaded directly to CMI storage in Norway stays within the controller's operations and needs practical safeguards, not Standard Contractual Clauses. Research assistants work under CMI instructions with confidentiality clauses in their contracts. The University of Nyaruma accesses pseudonymised transcripts through the restricted SharePoint site under the Art. 26 agreement; no separate copies are transferred.

**Retention and disposal.** Anonymised primary data is retained for ten years, administrative records for five years, and participant contact information for up to three years (as stated in the information letter). Recordings are deleted after transcription is verified, since the transcripts, not the audio, are the research data. Documentation of participants' agreement is retained as long as related data exists. Deletion covers all systems, including the partner's access, and is recorded in a deletion log.

### 4.2 Intellectual property and data ownership

Data ownership is shared between CMI and the University of Nyaruma, formalised in the consortium agreement alongside the joint controllership arrangement. Publications are open access under CC BY 4.0 and deposited in CMI's institutional repository. The document corpus consists of public records with no restrictive licensing.

### 4.3 Research ethics

The main ethical risks are professional and reputational: officials and frontline providers who describe patronage, absenteeism, or budget diversion could face sanctions if identified. Mitigation combines pseudonymisation at transcription, aggregation of quotes in publications so that no statement is traceable to an identifiable office holder, and the elevated storage controls in section 4.1. Focus group participants are reminded that confidentiality within the group cannot be guaranteed.

The primary ethical framework is the NESH guidelines. The project will consult the CMI Research Ethics Committee (advisory) in autumn 2026. The Sikt notification will be filed by 15 October 2026, more than 30 days before fieldwork begins in January 2027. Ethical clearance from the Kanewa National Research Ethics Board and a district research permit will be obtained before fieldwork. REK approval is not needed, as this is not health research.

## 5. Expected data and sensitivity

The project expects around 60 semi-structured interviews (roughly 15 with district officials and councillors), eight focus groups of six to eight participants, field notes, and a document corpus of approximately 300 public records. Classification is per data object, per CMI's four-tier scheme, and is detailed in Annex 1. Recordings, transcripts, contact lists, and field notes are Red; the official-interview subset is Red with elevated controls for the contextual reasons in section 4.1; internal working documents are Yellow; the public document corpus and anonymised transcripts are Green.

## 6. Processing, quality, and analysis

Interviews are conducted in English or Kanewan, transcribed within four weeks by the project's research assistants under confidentiality clauses, and translated into English where needed with back-translation checks on a ten per cent sample. Pseudonymisation happens at transcription. No recordings or identifiable transcripts are entered into consumer AI tools; any transcription or translation service must have a data processing agreement and a no-training guarantee. Quality assurance includes interviewer training and pilot interviews in Musenda during collection, and transcript verification against audio before deletion afterwards. Analysis uses [NVivo](https://lumivero.com/products/nvivo/) with a shared codebook reviewed at joint analysis workshops.

## 7. Documentation, metadata, and archiving

### 7.1 Documentation

Each dataset has a README describing contents, collection context, and known limitations, plus interview and focus group guides, the codebook, and a corpus catalogue listing sources and dates.

### 7.2 Metadata standards

Dataset metadata follows Dublin Core. Files follow CMI's naming convention (for example `2027-02-14_INT-MUS-OFF-03_transcript-pseudo_v02.docx`), organised by work package and district.

### 7.3 Sharing and archiving

Full sharing of the interview data is not feasible: even pseudonymised transcripts from two named districts carry re-identification risk for office holders. The project therefore archives anonymised transcripts (not recordings, which are identifiable by voice and are deleted after transcription) under controlled access at the [Qualitative Data Repository](https://qdr.syr.edu) (QDR); the per-deposit fee of approximately NOK 15,000 is budgeted in the proposal. The corpus catalogue, codebook, and interview guides are published on [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org) under CC BY 4.0. Metadata for all datasets is published without embargo; the QDR deposit carries a one-year embargo after project completion. Focus group transcripts are not archived externally because group settings prevent reliable anonymisation; they remain internal under the no-external-sharing pathway.

## 8. Monitoring and updates

The PI and CMI's Research Data Management Adviser are jointly responsible for keeping this DMP current. Scheduled reviews follow the Sikt assessment (late 2026), the project mid-point (early 2028), and archiving preparation (spring 2029), with ad hoc revisions for significant changes such as a shift in the political situation in either district. Updates are logged with version numbers, dates, and descriptions.
