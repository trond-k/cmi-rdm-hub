# Suggestions for the SIKT Notification Form — ATTACH Project

Based on the project description and application documents, here are my suggestions for each section of the form.

---

## 1. Personal Data Types

**General categories:**
- **Name** — participants will be identified during recruitment and interviews
- **Contact information** — needed for recruitment and follow-up with interview participants
- **Voice on audio recordings** — ethnographic interviews and podcast co-production (WP2 and WP3)
- **Background information that, when combined, can be used to identify an individual** — participants' migration histories, family situations, legal status trajectories, employment, and location may be indirectly identifying, especially in smaller communities like Voss or Øygarden

**Special categories:**
- **Health data** — the project covers welfare benefits, asylum seeker status, and mental well-being related to precarious legal status; participants may include refugees receiving social security benefits
- **Ethnicity** — the project explicitly studies Syrian and Ukrainian refugees and other migrant groups, and examines how national origin intersects with legal status
- **Political opinions** — possible, given that the project examines trust in state institutions and views on policy legitimacy
- **Religious beliefs** — religion is named as an intersectional variable in WP3
- **Criminal offences** — potentially relevant if participants discuss deportation orders, revocation proceedings, or interactions with police

## 3. Project Information

- **Title:** Attachments as mode of regulation: migrant inclusion in the welfare state (ATTACH)
- **Summary:** Use the project summary from the application (paraphrased for the form): *"ATTACH examines how migrants' continued residence and rights are regulated through criteria linked to their territorial connections and perceived integration in Norway and Denmark. Through policy analysis, empirical legal methods, and ethnography, the project investigates how attachment requirements are developed, implemented by bureaucracies and courts, and experienced by migrants, and what consequences they have for inclusion, equality, and trust in the welfare state."*
- **External funding:** The Research Council of Norway/Norges forskningsråd (NFR)
- **Academic level:** Research/PhD project (the project includes a post-doctoral fellowship and is a research project, not a student thesis)

---

## 4. Purpose

- **Primary purpose:** Research or student paper (this is a social science research project funded by NFR)
- **Description of research and necessity of processing personal data:**

> *ATTACH investigates how attachment requirements in immigration, integration, and welfare policy are developed, practiced, and experienced in Norway and Denmark. Processing personal data is necessary because the project relies on in-depth interviews with front-line workers and migrants (WP2 and WP3), ethnographic fieldwork including participant observation (WP3), and analysis of immigration case files that contain personal information (WP2). Understanding how individuals navigate legal requirements and how bureaucrats apply them requires direct engagement with the people involved. The project also co-produces podcasts with research participants, which involves recording voices and personal narratives.*

- **Other purposes:** Research dissemination through podcast episodes co-produced with participants; potential reuse of anonymized data for future research.
- **Total number of data subjects:** **100–999** (approximately 30 interview participants across WP2 and WP3 in each country, plus ethnographic contacts; the case law analysis in WP2 involves 15–20 cases per country, though these are existing files rather than new data collection from subjects directly)

---

## 5. Legal Basis

**GDPR Article 6 (general categories):**
- **Research in the public interest** — this is the standard basis for NFR-funded research projects in Norway

**GDPR Article 9 (special categories):**
- **Explicit consent** — for data collected directly through interviews and fieldwork, where participants are fully informed
- **Research in the public interest** — may also apply, especially for the case file analysis and re-analysis of existing data (REMIMO transcripts)

**Supplementary legal basis:**
- None of the listed exemptions from confidentiality appear directly applicable, as the project does not access health registries or patient records. However, if immigration case files are obtained through the Nordic Asylum Law & Data Lab, the legal basis for that access should be documented.

**Approvals:**
- **Other** — Approval from CMI's ethics committee (mentioned in the project description). Possibly also an assessment from the University of Copenhagen's ethics board for the Danish component.

## 6. Samples

I would suggest defining **at least three samples:**

### Sample 1: Front-line workers and caseworkers (WP2)
- **Description:** Front-line workers in the immigration, integration, and welfare sectors in Norway, including caseworkers at UDI, UNE, integration programme staff, language teachers, NGO workers, and lawyers.
- **How identified/contacted:** Through existing professional networks, institutional contacts, and snowball sampling. Approximately 15 participants in Norway.
- **Age group:** 18+ (no upper limit)
- **Included groups:** None of the special groups listed
- **Data processed:** Name, contact information, voice on audio recordings
- **How data collected:** Personal interview
- **Information provided:** Yes, written (information letter)

### Sample 2: Migrants (WP3 — Norway)
- **Description:** Ukrainian and Syrian refugees and other migrants living in Bergen, Voss, and Øygarden who are navigating attachment requirements related to residence, family reunification, welfare, and citizenship.
- **How identified/contacted:** Through existing networks, fieldwork sites, NGOs, and migrant organizations. Participants for podcast co-production are recruited during fieldwork.
- **Age group:** 18+ (all participants are consenting adults, as stated in the ethics section)
- **Included groups:**
  - **Vulnerable groups** — refugees and asylum seekers
  - **Persons residing in countries outside the EU/EEA** — possibly, if some participants have transnational connections, though the participants themselves reside in Norway
- **Data processed:** Name, contact information, voice on audio recordings, background information (migration history, legal status, family situation, employment), ethnicity, health data (if discussed), religious beliefs (if discussed)
- **How data collected:** Personal interview, participant observation, other (podcast co-production)
- **Information provided:** Yes, written (information letter)

### Sample 3: Persons in immigration case files (WP2)
- **Description:** Individuals whose cases appear in publicly available court judgments and anonymized case files from the Nordic Asylum Law & Data Lab, concerning revocation of residence, family reunification, and related matters. Approximately 15–20 cases from Norway.
- **How identified/contacted:** Cases selected from public judgments and the Asylum Law & Data Lab based on relevance criteria (cases involving attachment reasoning).
- **Age group:** 18+ (though cases may involve family members of all ages)
- **Included groups:**
  - **Vulnerable groups** — asylum seekers and refugees
- **Data processed:** Name (if in public judgments), background information, health data (if in case files), ethnicity, possibly criminal offences
- **How data collected:** Register (case law databases and the Asylum Law & Data Lab)
- **Information provided:** No — *explain:* The data comes from existing public court judgments and pre-anonymized case files. Providing individual notification to data subjects would be impossible or require disproportionate effort, and the individuals already have knowledge of the proceedings in their own cases. The case files accessed through the Asylum Law & Data Lab are already anonymized.

## 7. Third Persons

- **Will the project collect information about third persons?** **Yes**
- **Description:** Research participants (Sample 2, migrants) may mention family members (spouses, children, parents), employers, caseworkers, or other individuals in interviews and during ethnographic fieldwork. Front-line workers (Sample 1) may refer to colleagues or specific cases.
- **Personal data types:** Name, background information, possibly health data and ethnicity
- **Which sample provides information:** Sample 1 and Sample 2
- **Will third persons consent?** **No** — it is not feasible to obtain consent from all individuals mentioned in interviews and observations
- **Will third persons receive information?** **No** — *explain:* It would be impossible or require disproportionate effort to inform all individuals mentioned in passing during interviews and ethnographic fieldwork. The project will minimize processing of third-person data and anonymize all identifying information during transcription and analysis.

## 8. Documentation

**How can data subjects access, correct, or delete their data?**

> *Data subjects can contact the project leader Jessica Schultz (jessica.schultz@cmi.no) or CMI's data protection officer to request access to their personal data, correction of inaccurate data, or deletion of their data. Participants will be informed of these rights in the information letter provided at the time of recruitment. Requests will be handled in accordance with GDPR Articles 15–17. For participants in podcast episodes that have already been published, deletion of published content may not be possible, and participants will be informed of this before consenting to publication.*

## 9. Security Measures

- **Directly identifiable data stored separately?** **Yes** — a linkage key should be used, with the key stored separately from the research data.

**Technical and practical measures:**
- Continuous anonymisation (during transcription and in field notes)
- Encrypted transmission
- Encrypted storage
- Restricted access
- Multi-factor authentication
- Access log

**Where will data be processed?**
- **Hardware** — CMI's research server and designated secure infrastructure. The project description mentions CMI's IT Unit for secure storage of sensitive data.

**Data flow description:**

> *Audio recordings from interviews are made on encrypted devices and transferred to CMI's secure research server via encrypted transmission. Recordings are transcribed and anonymized, after which the audio files are deleted (unless retained for podcast production with explicit consent). The linkage key connecting participant names to pseudonyms is stored separately from the research data on CMI's secure server with restricted access. Ethnographic field notes are written in anonymized form and stored on the same secure server. Case law data accessed through the Nordic Asylum Law & Data Lab is transferred and stored according to the Lab's established security protocols. Only authorized project members have access to the data through multi-factor authentication. Partner institutions (Nordland Research Institute, University of Copenhagen) store their respective data on their own institutional secure servers in accordance with their data management policies and any data processing or joint controller agreements established for the project.*

## 13. Privacy Risk / DPIA

- **Will the processing involve high risk, requiring a DPIA?** **Yes**

**The processing has high risk because it entails:**
- **Highly personal information** — health data, ethnicity, religious beliefs, legal status, and migration experiences
- **Long duration** — project runs 2026–2029, and some data may be retained for documentation
- **Regular processing** — repeated data collection through ethnographic fieldwork over multiple years
- **Evaluation or profiling** — the project examines how bureaucrats evaluate and assess migrants' attachments (though the project itself does not profile individuals)
- **Innovative use or application of new technology** — podcast co-production as a research method
- **Matching or combining datasets beyond reasonable expectation** — the project re-analyses existing REMIMO data alongside newly collected data

**Consult data subjects or representatives?**

> *Yes. The project's stakeholder group includes representatives from migrant organizations (e.g., SEIF, Ukrainian Association of Bergen and Hordaland) and individuals with lived experience of the attachment requirements being studied. They will provide input throughout the project, including on ethical questions and data processing. Additionally, podcast co-production involves participants as active collaborators in shaping how their experiences are communicated.*

**Is the data processing transparent and fair?**

> *Yes. All participants receive written information about the project's purpose, what data is collected, how it will be used, stored, and eventually anonymized, and their rights regarding access, correction, and deletion. The project focuses on adults (18+) who provide informed consent. Some participants belong to vulnerable groups (refugees, asylum seekers), which requires extra care in ensuring information is accessible and understandable. Information letters will be available in relevant languages (Norwegian, English, Arabic, Ukrainian, Danish) and explained orally when needed. The research team has extensive experience conducting ethical research with refugee populations and will follow CMI's code of practice and 'do no harm' principles.*

**How is information provided?**

> *Participants receive a written information letter (in paper or electronic form) before giving consent. The information letter follows SIKT's template and includes details about the project's purpose, what data is processed, the legal basis, storage and security measures, participants' rights, and contact information for the project leader and CMI's data protection officer. For participants with limited literacy or language barriers, the information is also provided orally. For data from case files (Sample 3), individual notification is not provided because the data is obtained from public judgments or pre-anonymized databases.*

**Is the purpose clearly defined?**

> *Yes. The purpose is academic research on the regulation of migrant attachments in Norway and Denmark. Personal data is collected and processed solely to answer the project's research questions about policy development, bureaucratic practice, and migrants' lived experiences. Data will not be used for other purposes without additional consent or legal basis. The re-analysis of REMIMO transcripts is compatible with the original research purpose (immigration regulation and integration).*

**Are all collected data necessary?**

> *Yes. The project collects only data that is necessary to understand how attachment requirements function in law, practice, and lived experience. Interviews with front-line workers require information about their professional roles and perspectives. Interviews and ethnographic observation with migrants require information about their legal status trajectories, family situations, employment, and experiences with state institutions. Special categories of data (ethnicity, health, religion) are collected only insofar as participants share this information in relation to the research questions. The project does not collect data beyond what is needed for analysis.*

**How is data accuracy ensured?**

> *Interview data reflects participants' own accounts and perspectives, which is the nature of qualitative research. Transcriptions are checked against recordings for accuracy. Case law data is obtained from official sources (court judgments, Asylum Law & Data Lab). The risk of inaccuracy is low given the qualitative methodology, and participants have the right to review and correct their data.*

**How long is data stored?**

> *Personal data will be stored for the duration of the project (2026–2029) and anonymized at the project's conclusion. Audio recordings will be deleted after transcription and anonymization, unless participants have consented to their use in published podcasts. The linkage key will be deleted at the end of the project.*

**Responsibility, collaboration, and roles:**

> *CMI is the data controller. Data processing agreements will be established with Nordland Research Institute and the University of Copenhagen, depending on whether they are classified as joint controllers or independent controllers for their respective data collection activities. The Nordic Asylum Law & Data Lab has its own data governance framework. No data transfers outside the EU/EEA are anticipated.*

**How many/who will have access and through which systems?**

> *Approximately 6 researchers will have access to the data: Jessica Schultz, Cathrine Talleraas (CMI), Helga Eggebø (Nordland Research Institute), Narges Ghandchi, Sarah-Louise Mortensen (University of Copenhagen/CMI), and Jens Vedsted-Hansen (Aarhus University). Each researcher will have access only to the data relevant to their work package. Data is accessed through the respective institutions' secure research servers with multi-factor authentication and restricted access controls.*
