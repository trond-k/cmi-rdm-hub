# Clarify partners, processors, and data roles

Use this guide when someone outside the core CMI project team will collect, process, store, or access project data.

Do not start with the agreement name. Start with the role. The basic question is who decides why and how personal data is processed. That is the distinction the European Data Protection Board uses for controller and processor questions.

## The practical split

Use this table as a first cut:

| Situation | Main role question | What usually needs to be in place |
|---|---|---|
| A research partner co-designs the study and helps decide methods, access, or reuse | controller or joint-controller question | written agreement on responsibilities, access, participant information, retention, and what happens to copies at the end |
| A survey firm, transcription service, translation service, or cloud tool processes data on CMI's instructions | processor question | data processing agreement, security instructions, deletion or return terms, and transfer review if outside the EEA |
| A research assistant, consultant, or affiliate works under CMI's authority | internal-authority question, not a separate processor by default | contract or confidentiality clause, written instructions, and restricted access to only what they need |
| A ministry, NGO, archive, or other data holder gives or receives data under stated conditions | data-sharing or data-use question | written terms on permitted use, onward sharing, publication, retention, and deletion |

If the role is still fuzzy, stop there first. The wrong agreement usually starts with a vague description of the relationship.

## Settle these points before data moves

Before personal data is shared or access is granted, the project should be able to answer:

- who creates or holds which data
- who may access which files or systems
- where the data will be stored
- who answers participant or data-subject requests
- who reports problems or breaches
- what happens to copies, notes, and extracts when the collaboration ends
- what publication, reuse, or onward sharing is allowed

If those answers are not written down somewhere, they are probably not settled.

## Cross-border access is not a footnote

If personal data will leave the EEA or become accessible outside it, document the transfer basis before the access is granted. The European Commission's transfer rules point to mechanisms such as adequacy decisions, standard contractual clauses, and derogations.

That does not mean every research relationship uses the same mechanism. It does mean the project should not improvise after the fact. If a non-EEA partner, processor, or service is involved, clear the arrangement with the relevant CMI support function before the work starts.

## Common mistakes

- calling everyone a "partner" and never deciding who is actually doing what
- giving a service provider access before a data processing agreement is settled
- treating research assistants as informal helpers with only oral instructions
- promising access, publication, or reuse rights that the agreement does not support
- forgetting that partners and providers may still hold copies at project end
