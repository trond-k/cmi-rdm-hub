# Repository Strategy for Research Data at CMI

**Status:** Recommendation for leadership review
**Date:** March 2026
**Author:** RDM Adviser

---

## Summary

CMI needs a clear repository strategy that covers both quantitative and qualitative data, meets funder requirements, and reflects the realities of our research profile — where much data is sensitive, qualitative, and produced in partnership with Global South institutions. The current Norwegian infrastructure has a significant gap for qualitative data from sensitive research contexts. This document recommends a tiered approach using four external repositories plus the CMI internal repository.

**Decision requested:** Approve the recommended repository strategy and authorise the RDM Adviser to (1) initiate a DataverseNO partnership inquiry, (2) create a CMI community on Zenodo, and (3) explore institutional membership with the Qualitative Data Repository.

---

## The problem

CMI's existing repository guidance lists DataverseNO, Sikt, Zenodo, and OSF without clearly distinguishing what each is good for or acknowledging their limitations. In practice:

1. **DataverseNO requires an institutional partnership agreement.** CMI does not currently have one. Without it, CMI researchers cannot deposit data there. Two non-university research institutes (NIBIO and Nofima) have successfully joined, so there is precedent for an institute of our type.

2. **Sikt's research data archive is primarily designed for quantitative survey data** and has restrictions that make it impractical for most of CMI's qualitative collections:
    - Sikt does not accept anonymised qualitative data. They require documented participant consent for archiving and future reuse.
    - Most of CMI's existing qualitative fieldwork (interviews in conflict zones, governance research, migration studies) did not obtain consent for long-term archiving at the time of data collection. This retroactively makes the data ineligible.
    - Embargo periods are limited (maximum 3 years after collection or 1 year after deposit).
    - Sikt is strong for survey data and projects where archiving consent is designed in from the start. It should remain a recommended option, but with its scope clearly stated.

3. **No current option for qualitative data with mediated access.** CMI's most distinctive data — interview transcripts, ethnographic material, field notes from politically sensitive contexts — has no natural home in the Norwegian infrastructure. DataverseNO is primarily oriented toward open data. Sikt requires consent for archiving that most existing collections lack. This is the biggest gap.

---

## Recommended repository strategy

A tiered approach, matching repository to data type and sensitivity:

### Tier 1: CMI internal repository (all projects)

**Baseline requirement.** Every project deposits data or metadata internally for institutional accountability. This is unchanged from current policy.

### Tier 2: External repositories by data type

| Repository | Use for | Access model | Cost to CMI | Requires agreement? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DataverseNO** | Quantitative datasets, replication data, open research data | Open access (primary), some restriction possible | Annual partnership fee (contact for pricing) | **Yes — partnership agreement required** |
| **Sikt** | Quantitative survey data; new qualitative projects with explicit archiving consent | Mediated access — secondary researchers apply | Free (state-funded institutions) | Existing institutional relationship |
| **QDR** | Qualitative data — interview transcripts, field notes, ethnographic material, case studies | Mediated and restricted access | ~$600–1,200/year membership (5–20 projects), or $500–1,500 per individual deposit | **Institutional membership recommended** |
| **Zenodo (CMI community)** | Replication packages, code, open datasets, supplementary materials | Open access, embargo, restricted by request | Free | No — anyone can create a community |

### Tier 3: Specialised repositories

| Repository | Use for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **NIRD (Sigma2)** | Large datasets from Research Council of Norway-funded projects | Free for Norwegian researchers. Primarily open access. |
| **openICPSR** | Quantitative social science data, international/comparative datasets | Free. Open to all researchers worldwide. Strong social science heritage. |
| **OSF** | Pre-registrations, open research workflows, collaborative projects | Free. Good integration with publication processes. |
| **Discipline-specific** | When funders or disciplinary norms require a specific archive | Use when mandated. |

## Why these choices

### DataverseNO — the right primary repository for open data

DataverseNO is Norwegian national research infrastructure, CoreTrustSeal certified, and assigns DOIs. It is the default expectation for Research Council of Norway-funded projects. Sixteen institutions are currently partners, including two non-university research institutes (NIBIO and Nofima). CMI joining would give us:

- An institutional collection branded as CMI within DataverseNO
- CoreTrustSeal certification (increasingly required by funders, including Horizon Europe)
- DOIs for all deposited datasets
- Curation support and training from the DataverseNO team
- Compliance with Norwegian research infrastructure expectations

**Alternative considered: self-hosted Dataverse.** Not recommended. Running a Dataverse installation requires a Linux sysadmin (estimated 0.2–0.5 FTE ongoing), plus server costs, security patching, and version upgrades. The maintenance cost would far exceed the DataverseNO partnership fee, and CMI would lose the CoreTrustSeal certification.

**Alternative considered: Harvard Dataverse.** Free and open to all, but lacks Norwegian institutional branding and the CoreTrustSeal certification that Norwegian funders expect. Suitable as a fallback, not a primary choice.

### QDR — filling the qualitative data gap

The Qualitative Data Repository at Syracuse University is the only major repository purpose-built for qualitative and multi-method social science data. It accepts interview transcripts, field notes, ethnographic material, and case study data — exactly the types of data CMI produces that have no natural home in Norwegian infrastructure.

Key advantages for CMI:

- **Expert curation for qualitative data**, including disclosure risk evaluation — critical for data from conflict zones, governance research, and sensitive contexts
- **Mediated access** — depositors can restrict access and review requests, essential for CMI's sensitive data
- **Open to international depositors** — no national restriction
- **DOIs assigned** to all deposits

**Cost:** Institutional membership ranges from $600/year (Tier 1, 5 projects) to $1,200/year (Tier 3, 20 projects). Individual deposits without membership cost $500–1,500 per project. Fee waivers and discounts are available. CMI's likely need is 2–5 qualitative deposits per year, making a Tier 1 membership ($600/year) sufficient initially.

**Alternative considered: UK Data Service / ReShare.** Free and accepts international deposits, including qualitative data. However, its identity is strongly UK-focused, which may not be the right institutional fit for a Norwegian institute.

### Zenodo community — free, immediate, no barriers

Creating a CMI community on Zenodo costs nothing and takes minutes. It provides:

- A branded page aggregating all CMI research outputs
- Curation workflow (reviewers approve submissions)
- DOIs for all deposits
- Searchability through OpenAIRE, DataCite, and Google Dataset Search

This is not a replacement for certified repositories, but it fills an important niche: replication packages, code, supplementary materials, working papers, and any open dataset that doesn't need the heavier infrastructure of DataverseNO. It also provides an immediate solution while the DataverseNO partnership is being established.

### Sikt — retained but with clear scope

Sikt remains valuable for quantitative survey data and for new qualitative projects that build archiving consent into their design from the start. It should be recommended with its scope and limitations clearly stated, not as a general-purpose archive for all CMI data.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Immediate (no cost, no approval needed):**
    - Create a CMI community on Zenodo
    - Update researcher-facing guidance to reflect Sikt's scope and limitations

2. **Short-term (requires leadership approval):**
    - Contact DataverseNO (`support@dataverse.no`) to request pricing and discuss partnership for a non-university research institute
    - Contact QDR to discuss institutional membership and explore whether fee waivers apply

3. **After pricing is confirmed:**
    - Decision on DataverseNO partnership
    - Decision on QDR membership vs. per-deposit fees
    - Update all policy documents, DMP templates, and researcher guidance with finalised recommendations
