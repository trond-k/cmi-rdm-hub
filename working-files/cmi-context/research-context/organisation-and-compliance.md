## Key roles in research data management

### Research data support

- **RDM contact point** (rdm@cmi.no): central contact for research data management support. Provides templates, guidance, and training on data management planning, repositories, licensing, ethics, and data protection. Supports researchers in aligning projects with institutional and funder requirements.

### IT and security

- **Head of IT** and specialised IT officers: manage institutional IT infrastructure, storage, and security. Provide approved storage solutions, access control mechanisms, and incident response procedures. 

### Project management

- **Project unit**: project controllers and quality/finance advisers supporting project management and compliance.

### Research leadership

- **Research Directors**: ensure research projects apply data management practices correctly. Provide oversight, resources, and accountability for data management across projects. Approve Article Processing Charge (APC) requests where applicable.
- **Research group leaders**: each group has a group leader who oversees research quality within their thematic area.

### Principal investigators and research staff

- **Principal Investigators (PIs)**: ensure a Data Management Plan (DMP) is created and maintained for each project. Ensure lawful processing of personal data. Approve data access and sharing decisions. Confirm proper deposit or disposal at project close. Decide data classification and ensure appropriate handling and access controls for their project data.
- **Researchers and project staff**: follow the DMP, document data and methods, apply security controls, and raise risks or uncertainties promptly.

### Institutional governance

- **Director**: approves institutional policies and ensures resourcing for compliance.
- **Data Protection Officer (DPO)**: oversees GDPR compliance for personal data.
- **All staff and partners**: follow institutional policies and apply access controls correctly.


## Research Ethics Committee

CMI has a Research Ethics Committee (REC), established in 2019, consisting of at least three CMI researchers. The REC's role is **advisory, not approval-based** — it does not function as a gatekeeping body that must approve projects before they begin. Its main functions are:

- **Ethics awareness and training**: promoting a culture of serious engagement with research ethics, including annual staff-wide training.
- **Ethics advice on request**: researchers may voluntarily consult the REC for an assessment of whether a project or procedure complies with recognised research ethics norms and CMI's Code of Ethics. The REC can provide written assessments and recommendations for addressing ethical issues.
- **Misconduct investigation**: the REC serves as a point of contact for suspected research ethics misconduct and carries out preliminary investigations as described in CMI's Code of Ethics.

Consultation with the REC is voluntary, not mandatory. However, researchers should consider consulting the REC early in projects that involve ethically complex situations (e.g., research with vulnerable populations, sensitive political contexts, novel data collection methods).

For serious misconduct, CMI refers to the Integrity Commission (*Felles Redelighetsutvalg — FRU*) of the Research Institutes Common Arena (FFA), and as a last resort, the National Commission for the Investigation of Research Misconduct.


## External ethics and data protection bodies

- **Sikt (Norwegian Agency for Shared Services in Education and Research)**: notification required for projects that process personal data. Most CMI projects involving interviews, surveys, or other data collection from identifiable individuals will require a Sikt notification. CMI has an institutional agreement with Sikt for privacy services in research (*personverntjenester for forskning*). For the details of this agreement and operational guidance on the notification process, see `sikt.md`.
- **NESH (National Committee for Research Ethics in the Social Sciences and the Humanities)**: provides guidelines that CMI follows. NESH does not approve individual projects but sets the ethical framework.
- **REK (Regional Committees for Medical and Health Research Ethics)**: mandatory approval for medical and health research as defined by the Health Research Act. Rarely relevant for CMI — most CMI research is social science and falls outside REK's mandate. Projects involving health data, biological material, or clinical interventions should check whether REK approval is required.


## Funding context

CMI's primary funding sources, relevant because different funders have different data management requirements:

- **Research Council of Norway (NFR)**: approximately 33% of external revenue. NFR requires data management plans for funded projects and expects compliance with FAIR data principles and open access policies.
- **Norwegian Ministry of Foreign Affairs / Norad**: significant funding source for commissioned studies and evaluations. Data management requirements vary by contract.
- **EU framework programmes**: increasing share of funding. EU Horizon Europe projects require DMPs following the EU template and mandate open access to publications and (where possible) data.
- **European Research Council (ERC)**: ERC grants require open access to publications and research data, with DMPs following Horizon Europe requirements.
- **International donors and development agencies**: funding from various bilateral donors (e.g., GIZ, Sida, Danida). Requirements vary.
