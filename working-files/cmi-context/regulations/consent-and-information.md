## The information letter

The information letter is the primary instrument for fulfilling GDPR transparency obligations and documenting ethical consent to participate. At CMI, where the default lawful basis for research is public interest (Art. 6(1)(e)), the information letter **informs** — it does not ask for GDPR consent to process data. Participants are free to decline to participate (their ethical right), but their participation is not contingent on signing a GDPR consent form.

For the full rationale on the consent distinction, see `gdpr-positions.md`.

### Standard content

Every information letter for a project involving human participants should include:

- Who is responsible (CMI, the named researcher)
- What the research is about and why personal data is being processed
- What data will be collected and how
- The lawful basis: public interest (Article 6(1)(e)), with reference to the Norwegian Personal Data Act §8
- For special category data: Article 9(2)(j), scientific research purposes
- How data will be stored and secured (reference the classification tier — see `data-classification.md`)
- How long data will be retained (see `retention.md` for default periods)
- Who will have access (project team, named partner institutions if applicable)
- Participant rights (access, rectification, complaint to Datatilsynet)
- That participation is voluntary and they can withdraw at any time
- Contact details for the researcher and the RDM contact (rdm@cmi.no)
- Sikt's contact details as data protection services provider

### Topics to address where relevant

The information letter should clearly describe any of the following that apply to the project, so that participants are genuinely informed before choosing to take part. These do not require separate consent forms or checkboxes — participation itself constitutes consent when the participant has been properly informed (see `gdpr-positions.md`):

- **Audio or video recording** — if the research involves recording, describe this clearly so participants can make an informed choice about whether to take part.
- **Use of real names or identifiable information in publications** — the default is anonymisation. If identifiable information may be used, explain this so participants are aware.
- **Archiving data for future reuse** — if data will be deposited in a repository for secondary use beyond the original project, participants must be informed so their engagement extends to that purpose (see `open-science.md`).
- **Specific sensitive processing** — any processing that goes beyond the core research activity, e.g., sharing identifiable data with a named third party.

### Withdrawal deadline

The information letter must clearly explain the limits on withdrawal — specifically that withdrawal or objection is possible up to a defined point (e.g., "until the data have been de-identified" or "until [date]"), and that after that point, deletion may no longer be feasible. Each project should define a **concrete deadline** in its DMP and information letter. See `retention.md` for the full treatment of withdrawal, objection, and their limits.

**Action for the researcher**: draft the information letter during project setup. Use CMI's information letter template (to be developed) as a starting point. Submit to Sikt with the notification form for review.


## Oral information delivery

**Applies when**: literacy, language, cultural context, or security considerations make standard written information letters inappropriate or insufficient. Common in CMI fieldwork in rural settings, conflict zones, or contexts where documentation of participation creates risk.

**Recommendation**: deliver information letter content orally in a language the participant understands. Document that the information was provided through one of:
- A note in the research log recording the date, participant (by pseudonym if names are not being recorded), and confirmation that information was delivered
- An audio-recorded verbal confirmation from the participant (if recording is part of the study)
- A witness confirmation (a colleague or research assistant confirms the information was provided)

Written consent elements (for recording, name use) can be replaced with oral consent documented in the same way.

**Do not**: produce a signed consent form in contexts where this could endanger participants (e.g., linking a name to research on political opposition).

**Action for the researcher**: plan the oral information delivery script before fieldwork. Decide on the documentation method. Note the approach in the DMP and Sikt notification.

**Escalation**: consult the RDM contact (rdm@cmi.no) for high-risk contexts where even oral documentation might create risk.


## Translated information letters

**Applies when**: participants do not speak Norwegian or English, or a local language version would improve comprehension.

**Recommendation**: translate the information letter into the language(s) participants understand. The translation does not need to be a formal legal document — it needs to be clear and comprehensible. Prioritise clarity over legal precision.

**Action for the researcher**: budget for translation in the project proposal. Have the translation reviewed by a local collaborator or research assistant for accuracy and comprehensibility. Retain both the original and translated versions.
