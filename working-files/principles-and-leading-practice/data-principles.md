# Data Principles

*Trond Kvamme · 3 min read*

Four principles guide how CMI approaches research data — from how we think about data's role in society to how we manage it in practice.

---

### Data is political

Data is never neutral. Across the world, data is deleted, ignored, manipulated, or fabricated to serve political agendas. We see this in climate debates, health crises, economic policymaking, and in conflict reporting, where casualty figures are often disputed. In political debates — including in Norway in the run-up to elections — data are frequently cherry-picked or selectively framed to support competing narratives. What data we choose to collect, curate, disseminate, and preserve actively shapes political and ideological understandings.

This extends to how different kinds of knowledge are valued. Not all data are treated equally: quantitative and computational data, especially from STEM fields, are often privileged as more "scientific" than qualitative or interpretive research. This hierarchy is reflected in infrastructure — significant investment supports big-data repositories and computational tools, while few comparable resources exist for qualitative research materials. Such disparities affect what gets funded, published, and legitimised as knowledge.

For CMI, recognising the political dimensions of data means two things: careful attention to transparency and accountability when research evidence enters governance and public debate, and an explicit commitment to the **equal value of diverse forms of data** — curating both qualitative and quantitative materials as essential sources of insight.

For a deeper exploration of how data is manipulated, contested, and reclaimed globally, see [The politics of data](politics-of-data.md).

### Data is voice

Data represents more than information; it amplifies voices. Some perspectives gain extensive representation in datasets, while others remain marginalised or invisible. Datasets are not neutral — they determine whose stories are told and whose remain unheard. **Data is representation**. Given CMI's wide project portfolio and focus on development research, this creates a responsibility to adopt data practices that actively amplify underrepresented perspectives while upholding high ethical standards in collection, curation, and dissemination.

### Data is memory

Data is institutional and societal memory. Each dataset, transcript, or survey preserved today forms the foundation for future research. The loss of data means the loss of knowledge. If all of CMI's past datasets and instruments were accessible in a curated archive, they would constitute an invaluable resource for contemporary and future researchers. Yet, like most institutions, such systematic archiving has rarely been achieved. The Research Data Hub is made to change this trajectory, making CMI's data sustainable and accessible over time.

### Data is value

Data is what connects CMI's heterogeneous research portfolio. An RCT in Tanzania, ethnographic fieldwork in Myanmar, a policy evaluation for a Norwegian ministry — these projects differ in method, context, and audience, but they all produce data that carries meaning beyond the original study. Well-managed data is what allows CMI to move credibly between academic research and commissioned work, building institutional knowledge that compounds across projects and years.

This is not only about meeting funder requirements for openness, though it includes that. It is about recognising that data outlasts individual projects, funding cycles, and careers. The datasets, instruments, and documentation that CMI produces today are the foundation for questions that haven't been asked yet. Treating data as a core institutional resource — worth curating, preserving, and making findable — is an investment in CMI's future relevance and credibility.
