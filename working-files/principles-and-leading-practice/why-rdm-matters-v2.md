# Why research data management matters

*Research data management is not a bureaucratic add-on. It is part of doing research well: keeping your material secure, understandable, traceable, and usable over time. At CMI, where projects often involve fieldwork in sensitive settings, multiple partners, and diverse data types, the practical benefits start from day one.*

## More than storage

Research data management covers the full set of decisions and practices that shape how your data is handled across a project. It is not only about choosing where files go. It includes how you organise, document, secure, and eventually share or archive your material.

In practice, this means thinking about:

- what data your project will create, receive, or reuse
- who should have access to which materials, and when
- how data was collected, processed, or changed
- how identifying information is separated from research material
- what should later be retained, deleted, archived, or shared

Good data management turns a collection of files into research material that is governable, interpretable, and, where appropriate, shareable. These decisions are easiest to get right at the start of a project, but they matter throughout. For a structured approach, see the [core principles](core-principles.md) that guide practice across CMI.

## What counts as research data

The practical question is: what materials does your project produce or collect that someone would need in order to understand, verify, or build on your findings? That is your research data.

At CMI, this should be understood broadly. Research data includes not only raw material but also processed and working files derived from it, and the documentation needed to make it usable. Data are not neutral givens; they are shaped by collection choices, instruments, and context, which is why documentation matters as much as the data files themselves.

Examples include:

- interview recordings and transcripts
- survey datasets and codebooks
- fieldnotes and observation notes
- photographs, video, audio, and geospatial data
- translation files and coding files
- scripts, tables, and cleaned working datasets
- consent records and participant information
- README files, data dictionaries, and methodological notes

!!! tip "Check the gaps, not just the files"
    Many practical problems arise between the main data files. A dataset may be well stored but poorly documented. Transcripts may be organised, but translation decisions may not be recorded. A pseudonymised file may exist, but the linkage file may be stored unsafely. Good data management requires attention to the whole data environment.

## Four ways data matters at CMI

Data management at CMI is not only about secure storage and clear routines. Data is never purely technical. It shapes what becomes visible, whose perspectives are preserved, what the institute remembers, and what future research can build on. For the broader arguments behind these claims, see the [philosophical foundations of data sharing](philosophical-foundations-of-data-sharing.md) and the discussion of [CMI's institutional context](cmi-institutional-context.md).

### Data is political

Decisions about what is collected, how it is described, which materials are retained, and what can be shared all shape how research evidence enters public debate, policy processes, and institutional memory. At CMI, projects often work in politically or socially sensitive settings. Good data management supports transparency, accountability, and careful judgement about context, risk, and representation.

### Data is voice

Data does not only record information. It also affects whose experiences and perspectives become visible in research. Good data management helps ensure that qualitative, contextual, and less standardised forms of material are documented and handled with the same care as more formal datasets. Frameworks such as the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance emphasise that communities should retain authority over how their knowledge is collected, interpreted, and shared.[^1]

### Data is memory

If material is lost, poorly documented, or left dependent on one person's folders and decisions, future researchers cannot understand or build on it. At CMI, where projects often run across time, teams, and partnerships, good data management helps preserve datasets, transcripts, instruments, and methodological notes in ways that support continuity and later reuse.

### Data is value

Academic studies, commissioned evaluations, and collaborative field-based projects may differ in purpose and design, but they all produce material that can retain value beyond the original project. Good data management makes data more understandable, governable, and reusable over time. This is part of how CMI strengthens research quality, institutional continuity, and long-term credibility.

## Where to start

If you are beginning a new project, you should be able to answer five questions:

1. What data will the project create, receive, or reuse?
2. Will any of it be personal, sensitive, confidential, or contextually risky?
3. Who needs access to what?
4. How should the data be stored and documented?
5. What may later need to be retained, deleted, archived, or shared?

These questions do not require a finished plan. They require a conversation, ideally before data collection begins. The [core principles](core-principles.md) provide a framework for working through them, and the [file and folder naming guide](file-and-folder-naming.md) helps you set up practical conventions early.
