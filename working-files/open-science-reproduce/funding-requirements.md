## Background - RCN requirements

Integrating open science, data management, and ethical considerations into research grant applications is essential for ensuring compliance with the Research Council of Norway (RCN) while also strengthening research quality, integrity, and societal impact. These elements are evaluated as part of RCN's funding assessments and are increasingly aligned with international frameworks such as Horizon Europe.

See the [Open Science](open-science.md) section for a broader introduction and discussion of the concept.

Since 2023, open science has been explicitly incorporated into RCN's assessment criteria under the 'Impact' section of grant applications. Applications are evaluated based on:

- **Potential impact of the proposed research:** "The extent to which the planned outputs are openly accessible to ensure reusability of the research outputs and enhance reproducibility."

- **Communication and exploitation:** "The extent to which the appropriate open science practices are implemented as an integral part of the proposed project to ensure open sharing and wide distribution of research outputs."

RCN states that applicants that demonstrate how research outputs will be openly accessible, supporting FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) principles, and that embed practices such as pre-registration, open data repositories, and appropriate licensing (e.g., Creative Commons Attribution 4.0) align strongly with these expectations.

However, while data and research outputs should by default be as open as possible, they should also be as closed as necessary. Therefore, ethics is also a part of the requirements in RCN-funded research. Ethical considerations are assessed under the 'Excellence' criterion, which examines:

**The quality of R&D activities:** "The extent to which appropriate consideration has been given to ethical issues, safety issues, and the gender dimension in research content."

If certain data cannot be openly shared due to ethical concerns, security risks, or legal constraints, applicants should provide a clear justification for these restrictions. They should also outline how they will address key ethical challenges, including informed consent, participant confidentiality, risk mitigation, and potential negative societal or environmental impacts, while ensuring compliance with relevant legal and ethical frameworks. Ethical considerations should be context-sensitive, balancing international ethical standards with local cultural norms where applicable.

Although data management planning is not explicitly included in the assessment criteria, the RCN considers it a default expectation and an integral part of any research project. Through its policies, strategies, and general priorities, the RCN underscores the importance of effective data management as part of its commitment to open science and responsible research practices. A well-structured data management plan (DMP) helps balance these priorities, ensuring research remains as open as possible while adhering to ethical and legal responsibilities.

## Example texts and snippets

The examples below offer a flexible foundation for addressing open science, data management, ethics, and open access in research grant applications. A well-structured data management strategy is a key component of open science, ensuring that research outputs are as accessible, transparent, and reusable as possible, while balancing ethical, security, and legal considerations.

The snippets on data management can be used as a cohesive, long-form text or adapted modularly, selecting and refining relevant sections to fit the specific needs of a project.

For ethics considerations, segments are designed to be picked and combined based on the project's context, risks, and ethical challenges.

Note that while these examples provide a broad and adaptable framework, they should be tailored to reflect the unique setting, focus, and scope of each individual application and research project.

See also the Ethics and Research Integrity Resources segment below, for a list of selected national and international ethical guidelines. The guidelines that are most relevant to the project can be mentioned explicitly in the ethics segment of the application.

The statement on open access can be short. The included example suggestion is based on RCN requirements and the CMI Open Access Policy. Further brevity is possible; for instance, references to academic books can be omitted if they are not relevant to the project.

### How to address Open Science in funder applications - example texts and snippets

#### On data

Access levels will be determined following the principle "as open as possible, as closed as necessary":

- Open to everyone: Environmental, statistical, aggregated data
- Embargo period: Commercial research outputs (max 18 months)
- Limited access: Sensitive health data (registered users only)
- On request: Personal data requiring data sharing agreements
- Closed: Data with security implications or IP restrictions

**DMP updates**: The DMP will be a living document, updated and revised at key milestones such as midway through the project, after pilot studies or major data collection phases, and before data publication or sharing. Updates will incorporate lessons learned, address changes in project scope, and respond to evolving data management challenges and requirements.

**Accessibility of data/research outputs**: The data created by partner institutions will be accessible following their open access/science policies. [INSTITUTION] Dataverse, an institutional platform for data resulting from research, follows the principle of providing access on a royalty-free basis. Access modalities will follow Horizon Europe's Programme Guide recommendations related to open science.

**Interoperability**: [PROJECT] will follow pre-existing standards recommended by scientific communities such as: norm ISO 8601 for dates, ISO 19115/32018 for geolocalisation, EML for ecological data, Norm DDI for survey and social data, WHO, OIE and RDA recommendations.

## Ethics and research integrity resources

Ensuring ethical integrity in research requires adherence to international, European, and national guidelines that outline best practices for responsible research conduct, data protection, and participant safety. Below is a selection of key resources that provide frameworks, practical guidance, and regulatory expectations for research ethics and integrity.

These resources cover general research ethics principles, fieldwork in sensitive contexts, data protection, informed consent, online research ethics, and ethics in social sciences and humanities. Researchers should consult these materials to align their projects and applications with recognised ethical standards and to address specific ethical challenges relevant to their research.

### ALLEA (2023): The European Code of Conduct for Research Integrity

Serves as a framework for self-regulation across all scientific and scholarly disciplines in Europe. The European Commission recognises the European Code of Conduct as the primary standard for upholding research integrity across all research projects funded by the EU.

[ALLEA Code of Conduct for Research Integrity](https://allea.org/code-of-conduct/)

### TRAFIG Ethics Guidelines (2020): Developed for ethical research practices in migration studies

The guidelines from the TRAFIG project (Transnational Figurations of Displacement) cover topics like vulnerability of research participants, gaining qualified and voluntary informed consent, approvals for data collection, incidental findings, protection of personal data, photographs, secondary trauma, and safety measures for researchers in the field.

[TRAFIG Ethics Guidelines](https://trafig.eu/output/trafig-reports/trafig-ethics-guidelines)

### European Commission Guidance

The EC has published a series of guidelines drafted by experts at the request of the European Commission (DG Research and Innovation) and are aimed at raising awareness in the scientific community, and in particular with beneficiaries of EU research and innovation projects.

[Ethics and Data Protection (2021)](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/horizon/guidance/ethics-and-data-protection_he_en.pdf)

[Ethics in Social Science and Humanities (2021)](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/horizon/guidance/ethics-in-social-science-and-humanities_he_en.pdf)

[Guidance Note — Research on Refugees, Asylum Seekers, and Migrants (2021)](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/horizon/guidance/guidance-note-research-on-refugees-asylum-seekers-migrants_he_en.pdf)

[Identifying Serious and Complex Ethics Issues in EU-Funded Research (2021)](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/horizon/guidance/guidelines-on-serious-and-complex-cases_he_en.pdf)

### Association of Internet Researchers (AoIR): Internet Research: Ethical Guidelines 3.0 (2019)

Best practices for online and digital research ethics.

[AoIR Internet Research: Ethical Guidelines 3.0](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf)

### NESH Guidelines

[General Guidelines for Research Ethics (2019)](https://www.forskningsetikk.no/en/guidelines/general-guidelines/) — Provides overarching ethical principles for research practices.

[A Guide to Internet Research Ethics (2019)](https://www.forskningsetikk.no/en/guidelines/internet-research/) — Offers ethical guidance specific to internet research methodologies.

[Guidelines for Research Ethics in the Social Sciences and the Humanities (2022)](https://www.forskningsetikk.no/en/guidelines/social-sciences-humanities-and-the-arts/) — Details ethical standards tailored to social sciences and humanities research.

[Research Ethics in Ethnography/Anthropology (2021)](https://www.forskningsetikk.no/en/guidelines/ethnography-anthropology/) — Ethical considerations for ethnographic and anthropological research.
